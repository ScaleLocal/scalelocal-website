Arranging a time

There is no online booking tool here, and no calendar to click through. Appointments are arranged by speaking to someone. Call (978) 667-4595 and say what you need, or write to office@bgoguen.com and we will come back to you with a time. That is the whole process, and it is deliberate: the conversation that sets up the appointment is usually the one that tells us what the appointment should cover.

If you write rather than call, four things make the reply faster and more useful:

  • What you are filing — a personal return, a business return, a non-profit return, or bookkeeping you want taken over
  • Which tax year, and whether anything is already late or on extension
  • What changed since last year — a house, a marriage, a new state, a first employee, a business sold or started
  • Whether you have had a letter from the IRS or the Massachusetts Department of Revenue, and its date

Dates drive most of this. Forms 1065 and 1120-S are due 15 March; Forms 1120 and 1040 are due 15 April; estimated instalments fall on 15 April, 15 June, 15 September and 15 January. If one of those is close, say so in the first sentence and it will be treated accordingly.

If you are asking about a notice, the deadline printed on the notice matters more than any of our dates. Lead with it.

Existing clients: the portal

Documents, signatures, payment

Clients of the firm exchange documents, sign authorisations and settle invoices through the secure client portal. Sending a W-2 or a set of bank statements as an email attachment is not a good idea; the portal exists so you do not have to. It is also where signed copies of your completed returns stay available to you after the season ends.

Signature matters more than it might seem. Under the IRS e-file rules, the taxpayer must sign the authorisation before an electronic return originator may transmit a return — so nothing is filed until you have signed, and that signature comes back through the portal. Your paid preparer signs it too, and you receive a complete copy.
