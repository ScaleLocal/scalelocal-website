What the letters actually mean

Credentials after a name are not decoration, and they are not interchangeable. Two of the five here hold licences and credentials that carry specific rights, so it is worth setting out plainly what each one is.

EA — Enrolled Agent

A federal credential granted by the IRS, earned either by examination or by former employment with the IRS. An Enrolled Agent holds unlimited rights to represent taxpayers before the IRS. Thomas earned his in 2017.

CPA — Certified Public Accountant

A licence issued by a state board of accountancy. A CPA also holds unlimited representation rights before the IRS, and additionally may perform attest work. Brian has been licensed since 1981; Thomas became a Massachusetts licensed CPA in May 2022.

MST and MBA

An MST is a master’s degree in taxation — a specialist graduate qualification in tax itself, not general accounting. Brian holds one from Bentley College. An MBA is a general master’s in business administration; Thomas earned his at Bowling Green State University.

A preparer without one of these credentials does not hold unlimited representation rights. If you want the distinction explained at more length, see the guide on CPAs and Enrolled Agents.

The same people, year after year

Continuity

In much of this profession the person who knows your file is gone within a couple of years, and the next return starts from a folder rather than from a memory. That is not the case here. Dana Reardon joined in 2000 after working in the mutual fund industry. Sean Malone joined in 2000 as well, straight out of UMass Lowell. Both are still at the desk.

Continuity is worth something concrete at tax time: carryforwards, basis, depreciation schedules, the reason a particular election was made four years ago. Someone who was there when the choice was made does not have to reconstruct it.
