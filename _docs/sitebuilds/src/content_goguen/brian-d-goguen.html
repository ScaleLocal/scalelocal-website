What a tax degree is for

Massachusetts, specifically

Preparing a return and knowing the tax law are related skills, but they are not the same skill. Software will complete a form. It will not tell an owner which of two defensible positions is the better one, or why a Massachusetts return diverges from the federal return it was built from.

Massachusetts diverges quite a lot. A few of the places it does:

  • Most income is taxed at a flat 5%, with short-term capital gains at a higher separate rate and a 4% surtax above a threshold a little over one million dollars that is adjusted annually.
  • There is no Massachusetts standard deduction. The federal habit of taking the standard deduction and stopping does not carry across.
  • The state disallows federal bonus depreciation, and has deferred conformity to the increased federal section 179 limits for the 2025 and 2026 tax years — so a deduction taken federally may not be available on the state return.
  • A Massachusetts corporation owes a minimum corporate excise even in a loss year: $456 for the 2025 tax year.
  • The six-month state extension is automatic only if at least 80% of the total tax is already paid. Fall short and the extension is void, with a late-filing penalty on top.

Residents file Form 1; part-year residents and non-residents file Form 1-NR/PY. None of that is exotic, and none of it is guesswork either. It is the kind of detail a taxation degree is built around.
