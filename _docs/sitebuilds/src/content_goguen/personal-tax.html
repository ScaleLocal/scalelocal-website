Two halves of the same job

Personal income tax

A finished return is a record of decisions already made. By the time the W-2s and the 1099s have arrived, the year is closed and most of what could have moved the number has hardened. Preparation is the accurate reporting of that year, filed on time and signed by a preparer who will stand behind it.

Planning is the other half, done while a decision is still open. When to sell. Which account to draw from first. Whether a bonus lands in December or January. What to set aside so April is not a surprise. For most of the people we work with, those are one conversation held twice a year: once over the return, once over what is coming.

Where a return stops being simple

The first year of self-employmentIncome arrives with no withholding behind it. Self-employment tax, a Schedule C and quarterly instalments all appear at once, and the first year is the one where the shortfall is largest.Schedule C
A property saleBasis is the whole question — what was paid, what was added over the years of ownership, and what depreciation was already taken if it was ever rented. Both closing statements matter, the one from buying and the one from selling.Basis
Equity compensationRestricted stock, options and employee purchase plans are reported in more than one place, and the brokerage statement often shows a basis that omits the amount already taxed through payroll. Left uncorrected, the same income is taxed twice.W-2 and 1099-B
Two states in one yearMassachusetts residents file Form 1; part-year residents and non-residents file Form 1-NR/PY. A move mid-year, or work performed across a border, splits the income and raises the question of which state taxes what.Form 1-NR/PY
A rentalDepreciation is not optional, and Massachusetts disallows federal bonus depreciation, so the federal and state pictures diverge from the first year the property is placed in service.Depreciation
Retirement withdrawals startingThe first year of distributions changes what is being withheld, can make part of Social Security taxable, and often creates an estimated-payment obligation where none existed.Withholding

Any one of these is a reason to talk before the year closes.

The Massachusetts return is its own return

A federal return prepared well is not automatically a Massachusetts return prepared well. The Commonwealth taxes most income at a flat rate, taxes short-term capital gains at a higher separate rate, and gives no standard deduction — so a federal position built on the standard deduction does nothing on Form 1. Above a threshold a little over one million dollars, adjusted annually, a 4% surtax applies on top.

That catches people in unusual years — a business sold, a long-held property changing hands, a large distribution taken. The federal consequence is usually the one anticipated; the state one arrives uninvited.

Instalments, extensions and what lateness costs

Dates worth keeping

Income that arrives without withholding has to be paid for as it is earned. Estimated instalments fall on a fixed calendar, and a missed one is not cured by paying more later.

ObligationDue
Estimated instalments15 April · 15 June · 15 September · 15 January
Individual return, calendar year15 April
Federal return on extension15 October

An extension buys time to file, not time to pay. The federal extension moves the filing date to 15 October and leaves the payment date where it was. The Massachusetts six-month extension is automatic only if at least 80% of the total tax is already paid; fall short of that and the extension is void, with a late-filing penalty added to the bill.

The arithmetic of being late

Failure to file runs at 5% of the unpaid tax per month, capped at 25%. Failure to pay runs at 0.5% per month, with the same cap. Filing late costs ten times what paying late costs — which is why the answer to a return that is not ready is almost never to skip filing.
