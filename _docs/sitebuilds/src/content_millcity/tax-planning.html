A return is a record of decisions already made

Personal & business

By the time a return is prepared, everything it reports is settled. The entity was formed or it was not. The equipment was placed in service in November or in February. The roof was patched or replaced. Preparation is careful arithmetic applied to a year that has closed.

Planning is the same subject at a different point in time — the part where the decisions are still open. Nearly everything that moves the final number is decided before 31 December. That is the whole argument for having the conversation in October rather than in April.

After year end the work is documentation and characterisation. Both matter. Neither is the same as choosing a different outcome.

Estimated payments and the autumn conversation

Tax is paid as income is earned, in instalments through the year. An underpayment charge is calculated from the date each instalment was due, so a shortfall discovered in April has already been running for months. Writing one large cheque in the spring does not undo it.

There are safe harbours built on the prior year’s tax and on the current year’s tax. Whether either is within reach is arithmetic, and it can only be done while the year is still running, while withholding on a paycheque or a pension can still be adjusted to fill the gap. Income that arrives unevenly makes this sharper: a strong summer at a restaurant, a property sold in July. By early autumn the shape of the year is clear enough to work with and there is still time to act on it.

The questions a restaurant owner faces

Quick-serve & full service

Meals tax in Lowell is 7.00% — 6.25% state plus the 0.75% local option — filed monthly on Form ST-MAB-4 through MassTaxConnect by every meals vendor, with no volume threshold. That is a cash-flow discipline before it is a tax question.

Tips, and a federal rule Massachusetts has not followed

P.L. 119-21 created IRC section 224, a deduction of up to $25,000 a year for qualified tips, for tax years 2025 through 2028, phased out at higher modified adjusted gross income. It is a deduction, not an exclusion, and it is federal only. DOR TIR 26-4 lists “No tax on tips” with Massachusetts conformity of No, and treats “No tax on overtime” the same way.

Plan for this now

The same tip dollars can be deductible federally and fully taxable in Massachusetts. Withholding set with only the federal deduction in mind leaves a state balance due in April. Worth raising with staff before the year closes.

The rest of the list

  • The FICA tip credit under IRC section 45B, claimed on Form 8846. It cannot be claimed on tips used to meet the federal minimum wage in effect on 1 January 2007 — $5.15 an hour, frozen in the statute.
  • Service charges added to the bill are not tips. They are non-tip wages, and they behave differently in payroll and in the credit.
  • Who may share in a tip pool under M.G.L. c. 149 s. 152A. Anyone with managerial responsibility may not.
  • Whether the $6.75 service rate can be used at all, and whether the arithmetic reaches the $15.00 minimum every week.
  • Equipment bought late in the year, and whether it is placed in service before 31 December or after it.

The questions a landlord faces

27.5years, residential recovery period under GDS
45days to identify replacement property
180days to complete the exchange
25%maximum rate on unrecaptured section 1250 gain

Depreciation on residential rental property runs over 27.5 years under GDS with the mid-month convention, and land is not depreciable — it does not wear out. The split between land and building is fixed at purchase and follows the property for its whole life. Repairs and improvements are the recurring question: an expenditure is an improvement if it is a Betterment, an Adaptation to a new use, or a Restoration. That is settled by what is done, so the moment to ask is before the contractor starts.

Where the owner actively participates there is a special allowance of up to $25,000 of rental loss against non-passive income, reduced as modified adjusted gross income rises and eventually removed. Disallowed losses carry forward, and that stack is worth understanding well before a sale rather than during one. Real estate professional status under IRC 469(c)(7) has two tests and both must be met: more than half of personal services in real property trades or businesses with material participation, and more than 750 hours in them. Hours are proved by a record kept as the year goes.

On a sale, unrecaptured section 1250 gain is taxed at a maximum of 25%. A section 1031 exchange now covers real property only, and its clocks start at the closing — the structure has to exist before that date. Massachusetts taxes rental income at its flat 5% rate; short-term letting is a different regime, with a 5.7% state excise, a local option on top and registration with the Department of Revenue.
