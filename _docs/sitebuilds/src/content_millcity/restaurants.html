The meals tax comes first

Form ST-MAB-4

A meal sold in Lowell is taxed at 7.00 per cent: the 6.25 per cent state sales tax on meals plus the 0.75 per cent local option excise, which Lowell has adopted. Not every municipality has, so an operator with a second shop a few towns over can be collecting a different rate on an identical menu. The point of sale is set by address, not by company.

The return is monthly. Every meals vendor files Form ST-MAB-4 through MassTaxConnect regardless of volume — no threshold, no quarterly option when trade is slow. February with the doors shut for a week is still a filing month. Some larger meals taxpayers also make an advance payment ahead of the due date, worth checking once a location has grown rather than after a notice arrives.

Meals tax collected is not revenue. It is held for the Commonwealth.

Tips, end to end

What the employee owes the employer

An employee receiving $20 or more in cash tips in a calendar month from one employer must report them to that employer by the 10th day of the following month. Form 4070 is the report, Form 4070A the daily record behind it, both in IRS Publication 1244. Handing the booklet to a new hire settles any later argument about whether anyone was told.

What the employer does with them

Reported tips carry federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare, and appear in boxes 1, 5 and 7 of the W-2. That is unchanged for 2026. A large food or beverage establishment — in the 50 states or the District of Columbia, where tipping is customary, normally employing more than 10 employees on a typical business day in the preceding year — also files Form 8027. The count is tested per establishment, so a two-shop owner can be inside at one address and outside at the other.

The credit that pays some of it back

The FICA tip credit under section 45B, claimed on Form 8846, returns the employer share of Social Security and Medicare paid on tips. It cannot be claimed on tips used to bring an employee up to the federal minimum wage in effect on 1 January 2007, which is $5.15 an hour. That figure is frozen in the statute; it tracks neither the current federal minimum nor the Massachusetts one.

Service charges are not tips

An amount the business adds to the bill — a large-party charge, a delivery fee, a catering fee — is a service charge, and service charges are non-tip wages: ordinary payroll, outside tip reporting and outside the section 45B credit.

The tip deduction, and what Massachusetts did with it

P.L. 119-21

P.L. 119-21 created IRC section 224, a deduction for qualified tips of up to $25,000 a year for tax years 2025 through 2028, phasing out above modified adjusted gross income of $150,000, or $300,000 on a joint return.

Read the word deduction carefully. It is not an exclusion. Tips still run through the payroll, still appear on the W-2, still carry withholding, Social Security and Medicare. Nothing about the weekly payroll changes. The benefit lands once a year, on the employee’s Form 1040.

Massachusetts does not conform. DOR TIR 26-4 lists “No tax on tips” with Massachusetts conformity of No, and does the same for “No tax on overtime”. The same tips are deductible on the federal return and fully taxable on the Massachusetts return, in the same year, for the same employee.

For an owner that is a staffing conversation before it is a tax one. Staff have heard that tips are not taxed, and a stub showing withholding on reported tips looks to them like an error. One sentence — the federal break comes off your own return next spring, and Massachusetts does not follow it — settles the matter at the counter.

The service rate, and who may share a pool

The Massachusetts minimum wage is $15.00 an hour and the tipped service rate is $6.75. Both took effect on 1 January 2023 and neither has changed for 2026. The service rate may be used only where the employee regularly receives more than $20 a month in tips, and only where tips plus the service rate bring that employee to at least the full minimum wage. Otherwise the difference is the employer’s to make up. The test runs pay period by pay period, so tip records and payroll register have to agree before the run goes out.

The Tips Act, M.G.L. c. 149 s. 152A, decides who may receive a tip at all. Only wait staff employees, service employees and service bartenders may take tips or share in a pool, and each category requires no managerial responsibility. An owner-operator working the line six days a week may not draw from the pool.

Job titles do not decide it

Managerial responsibility is judged on duties, not on the schedule. A crew lead who expedites orders sits differently from one who hires, disciplines or sends people home early.

What the books look like

Quick-serve

Quick-serve restaurant owners were Scott’s primary clientele for years before he opened this office in the spring of 2018. These books have a particular shape, and most of the work happens daily.

  • Daily sales from the point-of-sale close, split by category — food, beverage, retail — not one deposit figure.
  • Meals tax collected posted to a liability, so the balance sheet shows what is owed on any given morning.
  • Comps, voids and discounts on their own lines. A rising void count is a management report, not a rounding difference.
  • Cash over and short recorded daily, so a drawer problem surfaces in a week rather than at year end.
  • Third-party delivery reconciled from the platform statement, not the bank. Deposits arrive net of commission, so booking the deposit as sales understates revenue and hides the commission entirely.
  • Food cost against sales by period with labour beside it, so prime cost is visible while there is time to act.
  • Payroll tied out to the tip reports and the service rate calculation, every run.

Bookkeeping is monthly, quarterly or annual compilation, including accounts payable and receivable; payroll is weekly or bi-weekly with the federal and state filings included. Both are set out at bookkeeping and payroll. A second location, or a move from a single-member LLC to an S corporation, sits under new business.
