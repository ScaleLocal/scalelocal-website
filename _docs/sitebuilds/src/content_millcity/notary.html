What the seal actually certifies

Licensed in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts

A notarial act is narrower than most people expect. The notary confirms that the person standing there is who the identification says, that the signature was made or acknowledged in the notary’s presence, and that the signer appeared willingly and understood that they were signing. That is the whole of it. The seal is evidence about the signing, not about the document.

Deeds, powers of attorney, affidavits, vehicle titles, parental consent forms, corporate resolutions and lender packets all reach the same short list of requirements. What a notary does not do

A notary does not verify that the contents of a document are true. Nothing in the seal vouches for the figures in an affidavit, the boundaries in a deed or the promises in an agreement. If a statement in the document is false, it is false after notarisation as well.

A notary does not give legal advice. That includes choosing which form to use, explaining what a clause will do, or advising whether signing is a good idea. Those are questions for a lawyer, and a notary who answered them would be practising law.

A notary also does not prepare the document, does not act as a witness to its wisdom, and cannot notarise a signature that was made somewhere else, out of sight, before the appointment.

The commission, the fee and remote notarisation

7year commission term
$1.25maximum fee for an acknowledgement

A Massachusetts notary is commissioned by the Governor and holds that commission for a seven-year term. The maximum fee for an acknowledgement is set at $1.25 — a statutory ceiling, not a market price, and one of the few numbers in this line of work that has stayed put.

Chapter 2 of the Acts of 2023 authorised electronic and remote online notarisation in Massachusetts on a permanent basis, after several years of temporary pandemic-era rules. The signature and the identification are still checked; the checking can now happen over an audiovisual connection under the conditions the statute sets. If a document must be signed remotely, raise it when arranging the appointment rather than on the day.
