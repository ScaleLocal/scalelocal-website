The shape is chosen once and lived with for years

Business

Most people arrive at formation with the order reversed. The name is settled, the space is signed, and the entity is treated as paperwork to be done at some point before opening. It is the other way round. The shape decides which return is filed, when it is due, whether the owner takes a wage, and what happens to the profit before it reaches anyone’s personal return. Changing it afterwards is possible and it is rarely cheap.

The formation work done here covers the entity itself, the operating agreement where there is one, the applications for federal and state tax identification numbers, and the registrations that have to be open before the first payroll or the first taxable sale.

An LLC is not a tax classification

State law

This is the most common misunderstanding in the whole of formation, and it costs money in both directions. A limited liability company is a creature of state law. Massachusetts recognises it, it separates the business from the people who own it, and that is the end of what it does. It is not an answer to the question of how the business is taxed, because federal tax law does not have an LLC.

A single-member LLC is disregarded by default and its activity lands on the owner’s Form 1040 — Schedule C for a trade or business, Schedule E for rental real estate. A multi-member LLC is a partnership by default and files Form 1065. Either can elect to be taxed as an S corporation, or as a C corporation, and the state filing does not change at all when it does.

So “should I be an LLC” and “should I be an S corp” are not competing options. They are two separate decisions, and the second has conditions attached.

The March pair catches new owners out in their first year. A partnership or an S corporation is due a full month before the personal return, because the owners cannot finish their own 1040s until the entity has issued their K-1s. A business that treats 15 April as its only deadline has already missed one. Each date moves to the next business day where it falls on a weekend or a holiday.

The late filing penalty on a Form 1065 or a Form 1120-S is charged per owner per month, for up to twelve months — so it scales with the number of partners or shareholders, and it accrues even where the entity made nothing and owes nothing.

Extensions move the filing date. They do not move the payment date.

The condition attached to an S election

Reasonable compensation

The attraction of the S corporation is easy to state. Profit passes through without a second layer of tax at the entity, and the share of it taken as a distribution rather than as wages is not subject to Social Security and Medicare. That is a real saving and it is why the election exists in so many small businesses.

The condition is reasonable compensation. A shareholder who works in the business has to be paid a wage for that work — run through payroll, withheld on, reported on a W-2 — before any distribution is taken, and the wage has to be defensible against what the same work would cost from someone hired off the street. An owner who takes the whole of the profit as distribution and pays himself nothing has not saved payroll tax. He has created an adjustment waiting to be found, with the tax, the penalty and the interest behind it.

An S election therefore brings a payroll obligation with it: a quarterly Form 941 or an annual Form 944, deposits on the schedule assigned by the lookback period, state withholding, W-2s in January. That work has a cost, which is why the election is worth the arithmetic rather than adopting because someone at the next table did.

Worth checking first

Below a certain level of profit the payroll and filing cost of an S corporation exceeds what it saves. The self-employment tax calculator gives a rough sense of the exposure on the current numbers. The decision itself is worth an hour in the office with the figures on the table.

The registrations are the part that gets left. A restaurant opening in Lowell is a meals vendor from its first sale, filing monthly on Form ST-MAB-4 through MassTaxConnect at the combined 7.00% rate, with no volume threshold to sit under. An employer reports a new hire within fourteen days. Neither obligation waits for the books to be set up.

Drafting an operating agreement is part of this work — a document about how the owners have agreed to run the business and how the money is divided. Scott is an accountant and does not practise law. Where a question is genuinely legal — a dispute between owners, a commercial lease, the purchase of an existing business, anything likely to be argued over — it belongs with an attorney, and it is better to say so at the start than halfway through.
