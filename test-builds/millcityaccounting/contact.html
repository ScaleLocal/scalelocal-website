Booking a time

By phone or email

A time is arranged the ordinary way, by telephone or by email, and it is confirmed by a person rather than by software.

  1. Call (978) 979-2904 during office hours, Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. Or write to scott@millcityaccounting.com.
  2. Say what the work is: a Form 1120-S for an S corporation, a 1065 for a partnership, a personal 1040, a bookkeeping catch-up, payroll set-up, a new entity, or a notarization.
  3. Give two or three windows that suit you. Saturday appointments are available and are arranged in advance during the week.

Meetings happen at the office. A first conversation about a business return usually needs the better part of an hour; a notarization needs only a few minutes, and you should say when you call what document is being signed and how many signatures it carries.

If the work is not a fit, Scott will say so rather than take it on.

What to have ready for a first call

The first call goes faster when a few documents are within reach. None of it has to be complete or tidy. The point is to see the shape of the year and the entities involved.

If you run a restaurant

  • The last business return that was filed, whichever form it was.
  • Sales reports from the point-of-sale system, with meals tax shown separately from food sales.
  • Recent payroll registers and the last Form 941 filed, plus a note of who is on the books as an employee and who is being paid as a contractor.
  • A list of the entities: the operating company, any realty trust or holding entity, and who owns what percentage.

Background on how quick-serve work is handled is on the restaurant page.

If you own rental property

  • The closing statement for each property, from the purchase.
  • The depreciation schedule attached to last year’s return — this is the single most useful page in the file.
  • Mortgage interest statements, the property tax bills, and the insurance premiums.
  • A rough list of what was spent on each building, separating repairs from anything that replaced or added to the structure.

The approach to Schedule E work is set out on the rental property page.

If you are neither — a personal 1040, a new company being formed, or a document that needs notarizing — bring last year’s return and call. The full list of work is on the services page.

Sending documents

Read before you attach anything

Email is convenient and it is not private. A message sits on several machines that neither of us controls, and it stays there.

Do not email this

Anything carrying a Social Security number, an EIN paired with bank details, a bank account or routing number, a driver’s license, or a full payroll register. Ask on the call which route makes sense for your file — dropping it at the office, posting it, or faxing it to (978) 856-3515 — and send it that way instead.

Questions, dates, figures and general back-and-forth by email are fine. It is the identifying detail that should travel another way.
