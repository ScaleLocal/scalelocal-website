What the seal actually certifies

Licensed in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts

A notarial act is narrower than most people expect. The notary confirms that the person standing there is who the identification says, that the signature was made or acknowledged in the notary’s presence, and that the signer appeared willingly and understood that they were signing. That is the whole of it. The seal is evidence about the signing, not about the document.

Deeds, powers of attorney, affidavits, vehicle titles, parental consent forms, corporate resolutions and lender packets all reach the same short list of requirements. Scott is a notary public licensed in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, and the service is available at the Kearney Square office during ordinary business hours.

What a notary does not do

A notary does not verify that the contents of a document are true. Nothing in the seal vouches for the figures in an affidavit, the boundaries in a deed or the promises in an agreement. If a statement in the document is false, it is false after notarization as well.

A notary does not give legal advice. That includes choosing which form to use, explaining what a clause will do, or advising whether signing is a good idea. Those are questions for a lawyer, and a notary who answered them would be practicing law.

A notary also does not prepare the document, does not act as a witness to its wisdom, and cannot notarize a signature that was made somewhere else, out of sight, before the appointment.

The commission, the fee and remote notarization

7year commission term
$1.25maximum fee for an acknowledgment

A Massachusetts notary is commissioned by the Governor and holds that commission for a seven-year term. The maximum fee for an acknowledgment is set at $1.25 — a statutory ceiling, not a market price, and one of the few numbers in this line of work that has stayed put.

Chapter 2 of the Acts of 2023 authorized electronic and remote online notarization in Massachusetts on a permanent basis, after several years of temporary pandemic-era rules. The signature and the identification are still checked; the checking can now happen over an audiovisual connection under the conditions the statute sets. If a document must be signed remotely, raise it when arranging the appointment rather than on the day.
