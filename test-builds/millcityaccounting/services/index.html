Six services, listed by the firm under two headings

Business & personal

Four are for businesses: tax preparation, bookkeeping, payroll and new business consulting. Tax preparation appears again on the personal side, alongside tax planning and notarization. The split is how the work is billed and how most people search for it. It is not how the work behaves.

For an owner-operated business the two headings are one problem. An S corporation return and the owner’s Form 1040 are the same money looked at twice, and the K-1 that joins them is only as good as the books it came out of. Payroll sets the wage figure that the corporate return deducts and the personal return picks up. A distribution taken in August changes what is owed the following April. Scott prepares both sides for most business clients, which is the point — the second return is not a separate engagement discovering the first one for the first time.

Two tracks

Before founding the firm in 2018, Scott’s primary clientele were quick-serve restaurant owners and rental real estate owners. Those two businesses have a shape, and the questions that come with them are not answered by a service list.

A quick-serve operator wants to know about meals tax, tips, food cost, and payroll on a weekly cycle with high turnover. A landlord wants to know about the land and building split, repairs against improvements, and what happens to a suspended loss. Both are cross-cutting — they touch bookkeeping, payroll and the return at the same time.
