Payroll.

Weekly or bi-weekly processing, including all required federal and state tax filings — and the deposit deadlines that carry real penalties.

Weekly or bi-weekly, with the filings that follow

Business

Payroll is processed here on a weekly or a bi-weekly cycle, and the processing is the smaller half of the job. Cutting the net pay is arithmetic. What follows it — the deposit of withheld tax on the right day, the quarterly return, the state filings, the new hire report, the year-end W-2s — is where payroll goes wrong, and it goes wrong quietly. A missed deposit does not stop anyone getting paid. It surfaces months later as a notice.

Of the businesses that run payroll, quick-serve restaurants are the heaviest. Fifteen people on a weekly cycle, tips reported monthly and turnover through the summer produces more payroll events in a quarter than an office of the same headcount in another trade produces in a year. The restaurant track covers the tip side of that in full.

Withheld tax is not the company’s money

Trust fund

The income tax, Social Security and Medicare taken out of an employee’s pay are held in trust from the moment they are withheld. They sit in the operating account, they look like cash, and in a slow month they are the easiest cash in the building to spend.

Where withheld tax is not paid over, the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty under section 6672 can be assessed against any responsible person who wilfully failed to pay it. Responsible person is a functional test, not a title — it reaches whoever had the authority to decide which bills got paid. The assessment is personal. It follows the individual rather than the entity, it survives the closing of the business, and it is not dischargeable in bankruptcy.

This is why payroll deposits are treated here as the first payment out, ahead of every supplier.

Federal unemployment tax is 6.0% on the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages, reduced by a state credit of up to 5.4%. For most employers that leaves an effective 0.6%, paid off early in the year on anyone working full time. The credit is the reason the headline rate is almost never the rate anyone pays, and the reason a state account left in poor standing is expensive in two places at once.

Massachusetts unemployment insurance runs on its own base of $15,000 of wages per employee per calendar year for 2026. New hire reporting is due within 14 days of the hire date, which in a restaurant hiring through June is a recurring obligation rather than an occasional one.

Form 1099-NEC is due to recipients and to the IRS by 31 January. Contractor details are needed in December, not in the last week of the month.

Deposit schedules, and the ladder for missing one

Federal

An employer deposits federal withholding either monthly or semi-weekly. Which one applies is not a choice and it is not based on how often payroll is run. It is set by a lookback period: the tax reported in an earlier stretch of quarters determines the schedule for the coming year, and the schedule can change from one year to the next without anything else about the business changing. An employer who was monthly last year and semi-weekly this year, and does not notice, is late on every deposit from January onward while paying the correct amount every time.

The quarterly return is Form 941. The smallest employers file Form 944 once a year instead, and that too is assigned rather than elected.

The penalty is charged on the deposit itself, and the step depends on how many days late it is.

Who is an employee in Massachusetts

Massachusetts decides worker classification under the ABC test at M.G.L. c. 149 s. 148B, and it is stricter than the federal common-law test. All three prongs must be met. Fail one and the worker is an employee, whatever the invoice says, whatever the contract says, and whatever the federal answer would have been.

  1. The worker is free from control and direction in performing the service, in fact and under the contract.
  2. The service is performed outside the usual course of the employer’s business.
  3. The worker is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, profession or business of the same nature.

The second prong is the one that catches people. A restaurant that pays a cook on a 1099 fails it immediately — cooking is the usual course of the business. An electrician who rewires the same kitchen does not. The federal test weighs a longer list of factors and may reach a friendlier answer; in Massachusetts that does not help.

Getting it wrong

A reclassified worker brings back pay, the employer share of Social Security and Medicare, unemployment contributions and the withholding that should have been taken — retroactively, and with the trust fund exposure above attached to the withholding.
