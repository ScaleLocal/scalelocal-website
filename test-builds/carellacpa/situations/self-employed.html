Home/Start where you are/Income with no withholding

Income is arriving and nobody is withholding tax.

A first 1099, a side business, a K-1, a large capital gain. The obligation to pay in during the year has moved from an employer to you, and it has four dates on it.

The shift

Two taxes, not one.

A wage earner has income tax withheld every payday and has half of Social Security and Medicare paid by an employer. Both of those disappear at once when income starts arriving without withholding, and they are separate problems.

Income tax is charged on the profit at your ordinary rates, on top of everything else on the return. Self-employment tax is the Social Security and Medicare contribution, and because there is no employer, both halves are yours: 15.3% in total — 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare.

Three details soften it. Only 92.35% of net profit is subject to the tax. The Social Security portion stops at an annual wage base, while the Medicare portion does not. And one half of the self-employment tax is deductible against income tax.

The self-employment tax calculator works through that arithmetic. Income tax sits on top of the figure it produces.

The dates

Four instalments, federal and state.

Estimated tax is paid quarterly, and Massachusetts uses the same calendar as the federal system, on Form 1-ES.

InstalmentCoversDue
First1 January – 31 March15 April
Second1 April – 31 May15 June
Third1 June – 31 August15 September
Fourth1 September – 31 December15 January following

Note that the quarters are not equal — the second covers two months and the third covers three. A due date falling on a weekend or holiday moves to the next business day.

The safe harbour

The single most useful rule here.

The underpayment penalty is not a fine for owing money at filing. It is interest charged for paying too little too late in the year, and it can be avoided even in a year of completely unexpected income.

Paying in at least the total tax shown on last year’s return — 110% of it if last year’s adjusted gross income was above $150,000, or $75,000 filing separately — protects you regardless of what this year turns out to be. Paying 90% of the current year’s eventual tax also works, but you have to know what that is, and in a volatile year you do not.

Which is why the useful moment to check this is the autumn, when there is still a January instalment left to adjust, rather than the following April when there is not.

If you also have a job

Withholding is treated as paid evenly across the year no matter when it actually happened. Increasing withholding on a salary late in the year can therefore repair an estimated tax shortfall in a way that a late estimated payment cannot. It is a genuinely useful piece of arithmetic and it is available only until 31 December.

Massachusetts

The state runs alongside.

Massachusetts taxes most income at a flat 5%, which makes the state estimate easier to size than the federal one. Short-term capital gains are taxed at a higher rate of their own, and an additional 4% surtax applies to taxable income above a threshold a little over one million dollars that is adjusted annually — which matters in the year of a business sale or a property sale rather than in an ordinary year.

Massachusetts has no state standard deduction; it uses personal exemptions and its own list of deductions instead, so federal and state taxable income rarely match.
