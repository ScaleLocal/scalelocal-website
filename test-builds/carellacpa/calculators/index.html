Seven calculators, run on the page.

A mortgage, a refinance, a loan being paid off early, a retirement projection, the tax on income nobody withheld from, an equipment purchase and a break-even. All of it is worked out on your own machine.

Why these seven

Property and borrowing first, because that is what people on Boston Road are usually working out, and then the two or three business questions that turn up in a consulting conversation. Each one is plain arithmetic written into the page itself: no borrowed script, no account to open, no cookie set, and not one of the numbers you type ever leaves the machine you typed it on. Unplug the network and they carry on working.

What they are not is an answer. Every rate, ceiling and threshold underneath them is rewritten each year, and not one of them knows your filing status, what Massachusetts does differently, or the rest of what is on your return. Get a rough size out of one and then ask the office what it means.

Buying and borrowing
What the house payment comes toPrincipal, interest and the escrow for tax and insurance on a fixed-rate loan, and what the note costs in interest before it is finished.→When a refinance pays for itselfHow many months of a smaller payment it takes before the cost of doing the refinance has been earned back.→Paying a loan down earlyThe scheduled payment, the interest it carries, and how much sooner the balance clears if you send something extra every month.→
Putting money away
Where the retirement account landsA projected balance built from what sits in the account today plus everything you and the employer put in between now and the finish.→
Tax nobody withheld
Tax on income with nothing withheldSocial Security and Medicare on profit from self-employment, with the half that comes back as a deduction shown separately.→
Running the business
What the equipment really costsWhat writing an asset off is worth at the rate you actually pay, and what the purchase leaves you out of pocket once the deduction is counted.→The point the business stops losingThe volume and the revenue at which a month’s fixed costs are finally covered, and what it takes to clear a profit on top of them.→

Describe what happened, and ask what it takes to sort out.

There is no fee schedule to quote from, because no two of these are the same. A first call establishes what the work actually involves before anybody commits to it.
