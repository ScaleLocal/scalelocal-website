Common questions

Questions worth asking first.

Twelve of them, answered without hedging. Where the firm does not publish an answer, that is said rather than filled in.

What does “four steps” actually mean?

It is the firm’s own framing: “4 steps to intentional success — Any 1 step gets you on your way. More than 1 helps you arrive!” The four are Income Tax Services, Financial Services, QuickBooks Services and Accounting Services, in that order. You are not expected to take all four.

Who will actually be doing my work?

One of two people. The practice is Tom Craig and Joe Brine, at one office in Wilmington. There is no bench and no offshore preparation.

Can you represent me before the IRS?

“Federal and State Representation” is on the firm’s capability list, and “IRS & state representation” is a heading on its income tax page. The IRS grants unlimited practice rights to attorneys, certified public accountants and enrolled agents; Tom Craig’s bio records that he became an Enrolled Agent and became licensed as a Certified Public Accountant. The representation page sets out the detail.

Is Joe Brine an Enrolled Agent?

The firm’s bio for him says he “continued his education by pursuing Enrolled Agent status (Licensed by the Internal Revenue Service).” That is the only credential sentence the firm publishes about him, it says pursuing rather than completed, and this site does not go beyond it. He is nowhere described by the firm as a Certified Public Accountant, and he is not described as one here.

Which business returns do you prepare?

The firm states that its software prepares returns for individuals, corporations, partnerships, LLCs and LLPs, trusts and estates. Business tax returns for sales and use and for business property are separately named under accounting services.

Can you produce financial statements for my bank?

The firm offers financial statement preparation and compilation. Its own description of a compilation is “based on client information provided and used primarily for internal use only, as no assurance is expressed.” If your lender has asked for a level of service above that, the office will tell you rather than take the engagement.

Do you teach QuickBooks, or do you just do the books?

Both are on the list. QuickBooks installation, instruction and follow-up support sit under step three; bookkeeping monthly, quarterly or annually, including QuickBooks training, sits under step four.

Is there a login, or a way to settle an invoice from the website?

No. This practice publishes neither, and this site implies neither. Documents and payment are arranged directly with the office.

What are the office hours?

The firm does not publish them anywhere, so this site does not state them. Call (978) 657-5272 and ask.

Where is the office?

Heritage Commons, 11 Middlesex Avenue, Suite 3, Wilmington, Massachusetts 01887. Directions and a map.

The email address is on a different domain from the website. Is that right?

Yes. Mail goes to info@tpc-cpa.com, which is the address the firm publishes.

Are the calculators on this site giving me tax advice?

No. They are estimating tools that run in your browser using the assumptions shown on each page. They know nothing about your filing status, your entity or your Massachusetts position. Use one to size a question, then call about the answer.

Still not answered?

It is a short phone call rather than a form. Say what you are holding and the office will tell you which of the four steps it belongs to.

Ask a question

Any one step gets you on your way.

Describe what you are holding — a notice, a shoebox, a first year of self-employment, a set of books nobody has closed — and the office will tell you which step it is and what the work involves.
