Calculators that open here, not somewhere else.

College saving, a retirement projection, a monthly house payment, the cost of equipment after the write-off, and the volume a new venture has to sell. Everything is worked out on the device in front of you.

Five calculators, and no trip to another website.

Twelve are linked from the firm’s site as it stands. Every single one of them opens on a vendor’s domain wearing a vendor’s skin, the firm’s name is nowhere on the resulting page, and the auto link opens the life insurance calculator by mistake.

These five open here. Nothing is fetched from a third party, there is nothing to sign in to, no cookie is written and no figure you type is transmitted anywhere. Turn the connection off and they still work, and they stay put if the firm ever changes web vendor.

Where each of the five comes from

Each one answers something the firm already puts in front of visitors. Nothing has been invented to lengthen the list, and the three links with no honest equivalent — bonus after taxes, total compensation, life insurance — have been left alone rather than approximated.

  • Four years of college, funded. A college savings calculator is on the firm’s list today.
  • What the 401(k) is on course to reach. Covers three of the links: the 403(b), the 401(k) and the Roth-against-traditional comparison.
  • What a house costs you each month. The home affordability link and the mortgage tax savings link both lead here.
  • Buying equipment before year end. Section 179 is on the list, and it is the one that most often gets asked about in December.
  • How much a new venture has to sell. Stands behind the startup costs link for a new business.

Any one step gets you on your way.

Describe what you are holding — a notice, a shoebox, a first year of self-employment, a set of books nobody has closed — and the office will tell you which step it is and what the work involves.
