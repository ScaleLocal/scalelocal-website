Tree Removal Permits in the Merrimack Valley — Town-by-Town Guide

Updated April 2026 8 min read By Bobby Black, ISA Certified Arborist

Tree Permits Vary Dramatically by Town

This is the question I get almost every time I walk a property: "Do I need a permit?" The honest answer is: it depends on which town you're in. Merrimack Valley permit requirements are all over the place.

I handle permit paperwork for clients all the time, so I've learned what each town requires. This guide breaks down the major towns in our service area. If your town isn't listed here, call your local conservation commission or DPW—they'll tell you what applies to your property.

Tewksbury

Public Trees

Public trees (on town property, in rights-of-way) require Tree Warden review. A public hearing is typically required unless the tree is an imminent hazard to public safety. The process takes time, so plan ahead.

Contact DPW: (978) 640-4440

Private Trees

Private property tree removal generally doesn't require a permit from the town, but you should check if the tree sits in a wetland buffer or protected area. Tewksbury has wetland protections that may apply.

Wilmington

General Requirements

Wilmington's Conservation Commission approves tree removal through either a Request for Determination of Applicability (RDA) or Notice of Intent (NOI) filing. Trees near or in wetlands require approval.

Safety Hazards

If a tree is an imminent hazard (storm-damaged, leaning, threatening a structure), you may be able to get Emergency Certification for expedited removal within 30 days. This speeds up the process significantly.

Replacement

Wilmington typically requires replacement of removed trees with native deciduous species. Plan for this requirement in your project timeline.

Public Trees

Public shade trees require a written request to the Tree Warden at DPW (121 Glen Road).

Contact: (978) 658-8238

Andover

Current Status and Changes

Andover is currently operating under its Wetlands Protection Bylaw. Trees in wetland buffers require approval through the Conservation Commission. The town established a Tree Committee in 2023 that is currently developing a comprehensive town tree bylaw, so requirements may expand in coming years.

Wetland Buffers

100-foot wetland buffers and 200-foot riverfront buffers protect trees in these zones. Trees in buffers require an RDA or NOI filing with the Conservation Commission.

Contact: CDPConservation@andoverma.us

North Andover

Conservation Commission Approval

North Andover's Conservation Commission has jurisdiction over trees in wetland areas and their buffers. Protected areas include the 200-foot Riverfront Area and the 25-foot No-Disturbance Zone around wetlands.

Private Property

Trees on upland private property typically don't require approval unless they're in a protected area. Check with the town if you're unsure.

Lowell

Tree Work Permit Required

Lowell requires a valid tree work permit from the Tree Warden before removing any trees. The Commissioner of Public Works serves as the Tree Warden.

Certified Arborist Preferred

Lowell prefers to work with Massachusetts-certified arborists or ISA-certified professionals. Having a certified arborist handle the removal or at least consult on the project smooths the approval process.

Chelmsford

Public Trees

Public shade trees need approval from the Tree Warden, plus a public hearing. The process is formal and takes time.

Scenic Roads

Trees 6 inches or larger in diameter on scenic roads require Planning Board approval. This adds complexity to your project.

Private Property

Generally no permit is required for private trees, except near wetlands (Heart Pond, Freeman Lake, Stony Brook have 100-foot buffers where tree removal needs approval).

Billerica

Conservation Department Review

Billerica's Conservation Department has jurisdiction over trees in wetland areas, including the 100-year floodplain, areas within 200 feet of perennial streams, and areas within 100 feet of other wetlands.

Public Process

Tree removal in protected areas requires a public hearing and abutter notification. Plan for a longer timeline if your property is in a protected zone.

Dracut

Tree Removal Permits Required

Dracut requires permits for tree removal per town bylaw. Check with the town's DPW or Conservation Commission for specific requirements on your property.

Methuen

Zoning Ordinance

Methuen's tree removal requirements are found in the city's zoning ordinance. Refer to methuen.gov for specific provisions, or contact the Planning Department for guidance.

Massachusetts Chapter 87: The Statewide Law

On top of local rules, Massachusetts Chapter 87 (the Public Shade Tree Law) applies statewide to publicly owned shade trees. If your tree is in the public right-of-way or on town property, state law applies. Private trees are generally not covered by Chapter 87 unless they're officially designated as public shade trees.

Wetlands: A Common Thread Across All Towns

Almost every Merrimack Valley town has wetlands protections. The standard is a 100-foot buffer around wetlands where trees cannot be removed without approval. Some towns have 200-foot buffers near rivers and streams.

If your tree is anywhere near water—a pond, stream, marsh, even seasonal drainage—there's a good chance it's in a protected zone. When in doubt, contact the Conservation Commission.

Timeline Planning

If you need a permit, the process typically takes 2–4 weeks from application to approval. Emergency hazard situations can sometimes be expedited. Plan ahead if possible—don't wait until a tree is failing to start the permit process.
