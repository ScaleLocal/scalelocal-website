Massachusetts Wetland & Environmental Regulations

Massachusetts takes environmental protection seriously. Wetlands, streams, and vernal pools are protected. Before clearing near these features, Bobby ensures your project complies with state and local regulations.

100-Foot Buffer Zones

Massachusetts protects wetlands within a 100-foot buffer zone. Work within this zone requires Environmental Notification Form (ENF) filing or a Request for Determination of Applicability (RDA) to the local Conservation Commission. Bobby is familiar with these requirements and coordinates with your local officials to ensure compliance.

Conservation Commission Filings

If your clearing project affects wetlands or buffer zones, you may need Conservation Commission approval. Bobby can advise whether your project requires filing, help prepare necessary documentation, and coordinate with town officials. This prevents costly delays and ensures no environmental violations.

Vernal Pools & Protected Species

Some Merrimack Valley properties include vernal pools (seasonal wetlands) or habitat for protected species like salamanders. Bobby's ISA certification and local knowledge ensure clearing respects these sensitive areas. If your project involves protected habitat, early consultation with town conservation staff is essential.

Bobby's Approach to Compliance

Don't leave environmental compliance to chance. Bobby will flag potential issues during your free estimate, advise whether Conservation Commission approval is needed, and help you navigate the process. His goal: a cleared property that's legally compliant and ready for your next step.

Land Clearing Questions

Do I need permits for land clearing in Massachusetts?

It depends on your property location and scope of work. If your project affects wetlands or the 100-foot buffer zone, Conservation Commission approval is required. Bobby assesses this during the estimate and guides you through the filing process if needed.

How much does lot clearing cost?

Clearing costs vary based on lot size, vegetation density, equipment access, and whether environmental permits are needed. A small residential lot with light brush costs far less than large commercial clearing or dense forest removal. Bobby provides a detailed free estimate.

What happens to the cleared material?

Brush is chipped on-site for mulch use or removal. Larger logs can be left as firewood. Stumps are either left or ground below grade. Bobby customizes material handling to your preferences—no waste, all options clearly discussed upfront.

Can you selectively clear while saving some trees?

Absolutely. Bobby identifies mature trees, oaks, pines, and other specimens worth keeping, then removes only the problem trees and brush. Selective clearing preserves your property's character while opening up space for construction or landscaping.
