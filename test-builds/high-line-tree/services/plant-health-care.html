Prevention vs. Reactive Treatment

Why Prevention Works

Waiting until a tree looks sick often means it's too late. By then, disease or pest populations are well-established, and removal may be your only option. Preventive care catches problems when they're manageable.

Regular tree inspections, early disease detection, and strategic pruning keep your trees healthy for decades. The cost of prevention is a fraction of emergency removal.

  • Annual or biennial inspections catch emerging problems
  • Pruning diseased branches stops spread early
  • Soil improvements prevent nutrient stress
  • Pest monitoring allows early intervention
  • Cabling prevents catastrophic failure

Reactive Treatment Costs More

Once a tree is visibly dying, treatment options shrink and costs rise. You're either paying for emergency removal and stump grinding, or attempting costly, often unsuccessful treatments on a failing tree.

Bobby's ISA certification means he can diagnose problems before they become emergencies. Early-stage emerald ash borer? Treatable. Late-stage? Removal is often the only option.

  • Emergency removal after a dangerous tree fails
  • Expensive treatments on far-gone trees
  • Property damage from fallen limbs or trees
  • Rapid spread of disease through your landscape
  • Loss of mature trees that took decades to grow

Plant Health Care Questions

How do I know if my tree is dying?

Signs include sparse or discolored foliage, bare branches appearing year after year, visible pest damage, oozing cankers, significant deadwood, or weak branch attachment. Call Bobby for an inspection—he'll assess the tree and recommend treatment or removal based on its condition.

What is emerald ash borer and is it in Massachusetts?

Emerald ash borer is an invasive beetle that kills ash trees within 2-4 years. It has been confirmed in 280+ Massachusetts communities, including North Andover and other Merrimack Valley towns. If you have ash trees, Bobby can assess them for infestation and recommend early treatment if needed.

Can a dying tree be saved?

It depends on the cause and severity. Early-stage pest infestations and fungal diseases can often be treated successfully. Advanced decline, deep cankers, or structural failure usually means removal is the best option. Bobby evaluates each tree individually to recommend the right path forward.

How often should trees be inspected?

Annual inspections are ideal, especially for mature or high-value trees. Trees over 60 years old or near structures should be checked at least every 2-3 years. Bobby can recommend an inspection schedule tailored to your landscape and tree species.
