When to Prune in New England

Best Time: Late Winter to Early Spring (January–March)

This is the ideal window for most trees in New England. Deciduous trees are dormant, so pruning stress is minimal. Wounds heal faster in spring, and you can see branch structure without leaves. Bobby schedules the majority of his pruning work between late January and March for this reason.

Oak Trees: ONLY Prune November–March

Oak wilt is a serious fungal disease spread through open pruning wounds in spring and summer. In the Merrimack Valley, where oak trees are common and valued, pruning between April and October is forbidden. Bobby follows strict oak pruning protocols—dormant-season only, sterilized tools, wound dressing when required.

Summer: Deadwood Only

Spring and summer pruning (April–September) should be limited to removing dead branches, branches rubbing or crossing, and hazardous limbs. Avoid heavy pruning in summer heat—it stresses the tree and invites pest and disease damage.

Fall: Proceed with Caution

Pruning in September, October, and early November exposes fresh cuts right as trees begin dormancy, slowing wound closure. Bobby avoids major pruning in fall unless there's an immediate hazard.

Types of Pruning Bobby Provides

Crown Thinning

Selective removal of interior branches to reduce density, improve light penetration, and reduce wind load. This is the most common and beneficial type of pruning for mature trees.

Deadwood Removal

Dead branches are pruned flush to the branch collar. They're safety hazards, visual detractions, and entry points for pests and disease. Bobby removes deadwood year-round.

Structural Pruning

Young trees benefit from structural pruning—establishing a strong branch framework and central leader. Early intervention prevents costly future problems and weak crotches.

Vista Pruning

Selective pruning to open sightlines, frame a view, or improve lighting into a home. Bobby balances the property's aesthetics with the tree's health.

Species-Specific Pruning

Red maples, white oaks, hemlocks, and other New England species have unique pruning needs. Bobby's certification means he diagnoses and prunes to each species' biology.

Reduction Pruning

Reducing a tree's size without creating stubs or flush cuts. Used for trees too large for their site, or to manage height near power lines or structures.

Tree Pruning Questions

When is the best time to prune trees in New England?

Late winter to early spring (January through March) is ideal for most trees. Trees are dormant, healing is fast, and you can see branch structure. Oak trees must only be pruned November through March to prevent oak wilt disease. Always avoid heavy pruning in spring and summer on oaks.

How often should trees be pruned?

Most mature trees benefit from pruning every 3–5 years. Young trees may need annual structural pruning to develop good form. Dead branches can be pruned anytime. Bobby assesses your specific trees and recommends a pruning schedule that keeps them healthy and safe.

Should I prune my trees in summer?

Heavy pruning in summer stresses trees and leaves open wounds vulnerable to pests and disease. Summer pruning should be limited to removing deadwood and hazardous branches. Save major structural and crown pruning for dormant season (November–March).

What's the difference between pruning and trimming?

"Trimming" usually refers to light, routine maintenance (hedge trimming, removing dead ends). "Pruning" is strategic branch removal for tree health, structure, and safety. Bobby performs ISA-certified pruning, which uses science-backed cuts to promote healing and prevent disease entry.
