The Stump Problem

Why Stumps Are a Problem

  • Create tripping hazards for kids and pets
  • Attract termites, ants, and wood-boring insects
  • Sprout unsightly shoots season after season
  • Reduce property value and curb appeal
  • Take up valuable yard space for years
  • Rot slowly—a stump can persist for decades
  • Make lawn mowing difficult and dangerous

How Bobby Solves It

  • Grinds stump 6-12 inches below grade
  • Removes root ball to prevent regrowth
  • Can grind same-day as tree removal or later
  • Leaves mulch chips for yard use
  • Reclaims your space for landscaping or planting
  • One visit, complete elimination
  • Safe, professional equipment operation

How Stump Grinding Works

🔍

Assess the Stump

Bobby evaluates stump size, root depth, surrounding obstacles, and access. This determines equipment and time needed.

🚜

Grind Below Grade

The grinder cuts the stump 6-12 inches below surface, eliminating the visible wood and preventing regrowth.

♻️

Chip and Remove

Ground wood is chipped. You can use it as mulch or Bobby removes it. Roots are pulled and cleared.

✓

Site Ready

The area is leveled and ready for planting, landscaping, or whatever you have planned.

What Happens to the Wood Chips

When Bobby grinds your stump, the wood becomes mulch chips that are perfect for landscaping. You can use them around plantings, in garden beds, on pathways, or anywhere you need mulch. Bobby can typically leave a pile for you to use, or he'll haul it away if you prefer.

Stump Size & Equipment

Small stumps (under 20 inches diameter) can be ground in an hour with a handheld or tow-behind grinder. Larger stumps require a wheel-mounted or self-propelled grinder. Bobby brings the right equipment for each job—no oversized gear for small jobs, and no underpowered equipment for large ones.

Same-Day Grinding or Later?

If Bobby removes your tree, he can often grind the stump the same day. If you have an existing stump, he schedules grinding separately. Either way, the goal is a cleared, usable yard space—fast.

Why Not Just Remove the Stump?

Stump removal (digging it out whole) is messier, slower, and more destructive to your lawn than grinding. Roots pull up soil, leaving a crater. Grinding achieves the same result faster, cleaner, and cheaper.

Grinding Depth Matters

Bobby grinds 6-12 inches below grade, depending on your plans. This depth eliminates future sprouting and allows you to plant a new tree, build a shed, or garden on the spot. The area fills naturally or can be topped with topsoil and seed.

New Tree Planting

Wondering if you can plant a new tree where the old one stood? Yes—once the stump is ground and roots cleared, the soil needs time to settle (a season is ideal), but the space is yours to use again.

Service Areas

Bobby serves all nine Merrimack Valley towns and schedules grinding quickly. For smaller jobs, he often fits them into the same week you call. Larger or complex stumps may take a bit longer to schedule but are prioritized as soon as possible.

Stump Grinding Questions

How much does stump grinding cost?

Stump grinding costs depend on stump diameter, depth of roots, accessibility, and how close it is to structures. A small stump (under 20 inches) typically costs less than a large one (40+ inches). Bobby provides free estimates so you know the exact price—no surprises.

Can I plant a new tree where the stump was?

Yes! After grinding and clearing roots, the area is ready. Let the soil settle for a season if possible, then add fresh topsoil. The space is clean and ready for a new tree, shrub, garden, or whatever you envision.

How deep does stump grinding go?

Bobby typically grinds 6-12 inches below grade. This eliminates the visible stump and prevents sprouting. The depth can be adjusted based on your plans—if you're planting a new tree, we go deeper; if you're just clearing the yard, shallower grinding works fine.

What's the difference between stump grinding and stump removal?

Removal digs the stump and roots completely out—messy, destructive to your lawn, and expensive. Grinding uses a machine to pulverize the stump below grade, leaving a usable yard. Grinding is faster, cheaper, cleaner, and leaves less damage.
