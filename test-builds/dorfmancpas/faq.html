Credentials & Scope

The license and the engagements

What does a CPA license actually mean?

It is a state license, not a job title or a membership badge — earned through an accountancy degree, a uniform examination, supervised experience and continuing education, and held subject to an authority that can suspend or revoke it. The consequence for a client is enforceability: a certified public accountant answers to that authority for the quality of the work and the confidentiality of the file. Anyone may prepare a tax return for money. Only a licensed CPA may issue a compilation or review report on financial statements.

Compilation, review, audit — where does one stop and the next begin?

Read them by what the accountant ends up willing to say, and they separate cleanly. Beneath all three sits preparation, where statements are assembled from the client’s records and nothing is said: no report, no assurance. A compilation keeps that assembly, adds a read-through for problems visible on the face of the statements, and produces a signed report that expressly withholds assurance. A review is where an accountant first commits to something — limited assurance, reached through analytical procedures and inquiry. An audit alone yields an opinion, and it gets there by testing, confirming and gathering evidence. Work and fee rise in that same order.

Review and compilation of small business financial statements are what this firm advertises. An audit is a separate engagement and does not appear among the six services on the services page.

Which one is my lender really asking for?

Often less than the letter suggests. A request for “audited financials” is sometimes a form phrase, and a bank lending against a small operating company will frequently accept a review, or a compilation with a signed report, once it is asked directly. Get the requirement in writing from the lender before the engagement is set: the levels differ widely in cost and the work cannot be upgraded cheaply afterwards.

What does “bookkeeping to trial balance” cover?

Exactly what it says. Transactions are recorded and the accounts are brought to a balanced trial balance that a return or a set of financial statements can be built on. That is the scope the firm publishes, and it stops there: not a controller function, not accounts payable administration, not the daily operation of your accounting system. See bookkeeping.

Can I keep my own books and have the firm prepare the return?

Yes, and that is a common arrangement. Bring the trial balance, or the records behind it, and the return is built from there. The constraint runs the other way: assurance work calls for independence, and ordinarily a practice may not express assurance on statements when it wrote up the records underneath them. A client cannot have both bookkeeping and a review of those same records from this firm. Tax return preparation is not an assurance engagement, so it is unaffected.

Filing & Payment

Deadlines, extensions and what goes wrong

Who files a Form 1041, and when does a trust need one?

Form 1041 is the income tax return of an estate or a trust — a separate taxpayer from the person who created it and from the beneficiaries. The trustee or personal representative files it. Whether a particular trust must file in a given year depends on its terms, income and status: a facts question rather than a rule of thumb. Trusts are within the scope the firm advertises; see tax returns.

If I extend, when is the money actually due?

On the original date, and this is the single most expensive misunderstanding in the calendar. A federal extension moves the filing date to 15 October and leaves the payment date exactly where it was. Massachusetts attaches a condition to its six-month extension: it is automatic only where at least 80% of the total tax has already been paid. Fall under that threshold and the extension is void, which puts a late-filing penalty on top of the balance owed.

What if the return is ready but the money is not?

File anyway, on time. Failure to file costs 5% of the unpaid tax per month, capped at 25%. Failure to pay costs 0.5% per month, capped at the same 25%. Filing late costs ten times what paying late costs, so a return filed on time with a partial payment is always the cheaper position than a return held back until the money is found.

Is a Massachusetts corporation safe if it lost money?

No. Losing money for the year does not relieve a Massachusetts corporation of the minimum corporate excise, which is $456 for the 2025 tax year. Two depreciation rules pull the same way: the state does not follow federal bonus depreciation at all, and for 2025 and 2026 it has held back from conforming to the raised federal section 179 limits. An equipment purchase can therefore land very differently on the state return than on the federal one.

How long should I keep records?

Key retention to the assessment period rather than to a habit. The normal assessment window is three years. It stretches to six where a return leaves out gross income amounting to more than 25% of the gross income it does report, and it never closes at all where no return was filed. Anything supporting basis in property — purchase, improvements, depreciation — should be kept for as long as the property is held and for the assessment period after it is sold.

Working With The Firm

Getting started, and getting caught up

Will the firm take on a business that is behind?

It is worth the conversation. Unfiled years are a known problem with a known order of operations: establish which returns are open, get the books to a trial balance for each year, file oldest first, and deal with the balance due once the numbers are real. The unlimited assessment period on an unfiled return is the reason to start rather than wait. Whether the firm can take the work depends on its size and the season it arrives in, and that is answered on the first call.

What should I bring to a first appointment?

Prior-year returns, federal and state. The books, or the trial balance if you have one. Bank and loan statements. Payroll filings if there are employees. Trust or entity documents where they exist. Above all, every notice received during the year, opened or not — a notice carries a deadline, and that is usually the first thing to handle. See arranging an appointment.

Do I have to sign something before the return is filed electronically?

Yes. The IRS e-file rules make the taxpayer’s signature on the authorization a precondition: without it, an electronic return originator may not transmit the return. The paid preparer signs the same authorization and provides the taxpayer with a complete copy. No return leaves this office on a verbal approval.

Are my records confidential?

Confidentiality attaches to the CPA license as an enforceable professional obligation. Information is not released to a lender, a broker, a family member or a prospective buyer without the client’s authorization, and requests arriving with legal compulsion are handled as such and disclosed to the client.
