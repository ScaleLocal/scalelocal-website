The Firm

A family-owned firm, formed in 2008

Dorfman & Dorfman, CPAs is a small accounting firm owned by Marvin H. Dorfman, CPA and Estee C. Dorfman, CPA, MSA. Two certified public accountants, one office at 402 Main Street in Wilmington, and a practice built out of two working histories that joined in 2008.

The firm is family-owned. The consequence worth stating is one of scale rather than sentiment: two Certified Public Accountants, one office on Main Street, and a capacity that is fixed rather than elastic. What can be taken on in February follows from that arithmetic and from nothing else.

Size, Stated Plainly

What a two-person firm means

Two things follow from the size, and a client is better served hearing both before an engagement starts than after.

The first is that the person who answers the telephone is the person doing the work. A question about a depreciation schedule or a Massachusetts adjustment does not travel down to an unnamed preparer and back. Files are known rather than looked up. Nothing is handed to a seasonal contractor and returned for signature.

The second is that there is no depth of bench. A firm of this size has no separate specialty group to consult on an unusual international question, no second team standing by in the last week of March, and no capacity to absorb an engagement that needs a dozen people. Larger firms exist because that work exists and is worth doing well. When a matter belongs with a larger organization, or with an attorney, the firm says so and says it early.

On independence

Independence is what assurance work rests on, and writing up the records is what compromises it. One firm therefore cannot ordinarily express assurance on statements drawn from books it kept itself — which is why bookkeeping and a review of those same records are not available together here. That constraint is professional, not commercial, and it is settled at the outset.

The services page states the scope of each engagement in the firm’s own words, including where a service stops.

Confidentiality

An obligation, not a policy

A policy is something a business writes and can rewrite. What binds a certified public accountant is different in kind: confidentiality is an enforceable professional obligation attaching to the license itself, and a breach of it is a matter for the licensing authority, not for customer service.

In practice this means client information is not discussed outside the engagement, is not used for any purpose other than the work, and is not released to a third party — a lender, a broker, a family member, a prospective buyer — without the client’s authorization. Requests that arrive with legal compulsion are handled as such and the client is told.

Records are held by two people in one office. There is no outsourced preparation pool and no offshore processing.

How An Engagement Runs

First call to filing

  1. The first conversation. What the entity is, what the year looked like, which returns are due and when, and whether the work belongs here. Call (781) 780-7069 ext. 11 or write to estee@dorfman-cpas.com.
  2. Scope in writing. What service is being provided, what it is not, and what the firm will rely on the client to supply. A compilation and a review are different engagements with different reports; the difference is fixed here rather than at delivery.
  3. Records. Prior-year returns, the trial balance or the books behind it, statements, payroll filings, and the notices that arrived during the year.
  4. Preparation and questions. Questions are asked as they arise. Anything that changes the number is raised before it is filed, not explained afterwards.
  5. Review with the client. The draft is walked through: what drove the result, what the balance due or refund is, and what the installments for the coming year look like.
  6. Signature and transmission. Under IRS e-file rules the taxpayer signs the authorization before the return may be transmitted. The paid preparer signs it as well, and the taxpayer receives a complete copy of the return.

Common questions about timing, extensions and records are answered on the frequently asked questions page.
