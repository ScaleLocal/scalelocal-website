Examiner’s discipline

What a Principal Examiner is trained to do

An examiner’s job, stated generally, is to read records and decide whether they support what has been asserted. Not whether the assertion sounds plausible, and not whether the person making it seems credible — whether the documents behind it hold. The question is always the same one: show me.

That is the identical question a tax authority asks on examination, and the identical question a bank asks before it lends against a set of financial statements. A small business’s books either answer it or they do not.

The MSA

A specialist degree, not a general one

MSA stands for Master of Science in Accounting. It is a graduate degree in the subject itself rather than a broad business qualification, which is why it appears after the CPA rather than in place of it. The CPA is a state licence; the MSA is coursework at depth.

Read the letters

CPA is a licence to practise. MSA is a master’s degree in accounting. They answer different questions and neither substitutes for the other.

Bentley College and Suffolk University are both named on the firm’s site. Marvin H. Dorfman, who formed the firm in 2008, also attended Bentley College; his own page is here.
