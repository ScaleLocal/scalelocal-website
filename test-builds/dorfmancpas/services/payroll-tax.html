Service

Payroll tax returns.

Federal and state. The most dangerous liability a small business carries, because it is money withheld on somebody else’s behalf.

Published scope

Payroll tax returns, federal and state

Dorfman & Dorfman, CPAs publishes this service as “Payroll Tax Returns, Federal and State”. The noun is returns: preparation and filing of the returns that report payroll taxes federally and to the state. It is not the running of a payroll.

Running a payroll means calculating gross pay and withholding, producing the pay, and depositing the tax on the schedule that applies. The returns report it afterwards — the accounting to each authority of what was withheld, what is owed and what has been deposited.

A business that uses a payroll service, or runs payroll in–house, still has these returns to file.

The filing rhythm

What gets filed, and how often

Payroll tax reporting runs on a calendar of its own, separate from the income tax year and busier. An employer with one employee is in the same rhythm as one with forty.

01Quarterly federal returnFiled after each calendar quarter closes. Wages, income tax withheld, and social security and medicare — employee share and employer match — against the deposits already made.Quarterly
02Annual federal unemployment returnAn employer–only tax, filed once after the year closes. Nothing is withheld from the employee for it, which is why it is most often forgotten.Annual
03State withholding returnsMassachusetts taxes most income at a flat 5% with no state standard deduction, which makes the arithmetic mechanical. Frequency follows the size of the withholding.State
04State unemployment reportingQuarterly, on a wage base and experience rate specific to the employer. A separate account and deadline.State
05Year–end wage statementsA statement of wages and withholding to each employee, and a transmittal to the government. These must agree with the quarterly returns already filed.Year end

That agreement is where most of the year’s errors surface. The agencies reconcile quarterly returns against annual wage statements as routine, and a mismatch generates a notice without anyone deciding to look.

Money held for others

Why payroll tax is the most dangerous liability a small business carries

Most of what a business owes is its own money: rent, suppliers, a loan, its own income tax. Payroll withholding is not. The income tax withheld from an employee’s pay, and the employee’s share of social security and medicare, are the employee’s money — taken from somebody else’s wages and held until handed on.

That changes the character of the debt, and the consequences reach past the company:

  • The withheld portion can be assessed personally against individuals who had authority over which bills were paid, and does not disappear when the company does.
  • Collection is faster and less negotiable than on other balances, because the money was never the employer’s.
  • Late deposits carry their own penalty, on a scale that rises with the days late and again once payment has been demanded, charged on top of any penalty on the return.

The failure is rarely theft. It is a cash shortage in a bad month, and payroll tax is the one bill nobody phones about until much later. Borrowing from it is the most expensive credit a small business can arrange.

File even if short

Filing and paying are two separate obligations

When the money is short, the instinct is to hold the return back until it can go out with a payment. The arithmetic is not close.

PenaltyRate
Failure to file5% of unpaid tax per month, capped at 25%
Failure to pay0.5% per month, same 25% cap

Filing late costs ten times what paying late costs. A return filed on time with a partial payment, or none at all, avoids the larger penalty entirely and leaves only the smaller one running against a balance that is fixed and disclosed.

An unfiled return does worse than accrue a penalty. The assessment window — three years normally, six where a return omits gross income exceeding 25% of the gross income stated — never starts where no return was filed.

Preparing the returns

What the work needs from the business

The returns are prepared from records the business already has: a payroll register showing gross pay, each withholding and each employer tax by employee; every deposit, with date and amount; the states in which employees worked; and any agency notice, unopened ones included.

Two things reliably slow the work down. Payroll recorded from the net cash that left the bank understates wages and omits withholding and employer taxes. A worker treated as a contractor whose arrangement looks like employment is better settled before returns are filed than after.
