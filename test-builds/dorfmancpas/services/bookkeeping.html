Service

Bookkeeping to trial balance.

The scope is stated plainly, and so is what it buys: books that reconcile to something outside themselves.

The published scope

Bookkeeping services to trial balance

Dorfman & Dorfman, CPAs publishes the service as “Bookkeeping Services to trial balance”. The last three words are the scope, and they are worth reading literally.

A trial balance lists every account in the general ledger with its balance at a point in time, debits in one column and credits in the other. When the columns agree, the ledger is internally consistent: every entry has been recorded on both sides. That agreement is the finishing line for this engagement. It is not a statement that the numbers are right — a ledger can balance perfectly and still be wrong in six places, because two errors of the same size on opposite sides cancel.

What it does produce is a closed, ordered set of books that a return or a financial statement can be built from.

Chart of accounts

The design decision that governs everything after it

Most bookkeeping problems are not posting errors. They are structural: the chart of accounts was never designed, it accumulated. Accounts were added whenever a transaction did not fit, and after four years there are three that mean the same thing and one that means four things. The chart is the one part of a bookkeeping system that is genuinely a design decision, and it fixes what questions the books can answer later without a rebuild.

01Tax lines firstAccounts that map onto return lines remove a reclassification step every year, permanently.Return
02Separate the ownerDistinct accounts for contributions, draws and any loan to or from the owner. Balance sheet items, never expenses.Equity
03Liabilities for money heldSales tax collected and payroll withholding sit on the balance sheet until they are remitted.Held
04Resist granularityDetail nobody reads costs posting time every month and buys nothing at year end.Restraint

Changing the chart mid–year is possible but costly, since the comparative figures move with it. The cheapest time to settle it is before a year’s first entry.

Monthly reconciliation

What reconciling monthly buys beyond the return

Reconciliation means agreeing a ledger balance to a statement produced by somebody outside the business — a bank, a card issuer, a lender, a payroll service. It is the only routine step in bookkeeping where an outside party is the authority. A business that reconciles once a year does it under deadline pressure, against records twelve months cold. Monthly reconciliation produces three things the annual approach cannot:

  • Errors surface while the document, the email and the memory of the transaction all still exist.
  • Duplicate payments, unrecorded fees and cheques that never cleared are caught while something can still be done about them.
  • Estimated instalments on 15 April, 15 June, 15 September and 15 January can be set against real figures rather than last year’s guess carried forward.

The items left over — deposits in transit, outstanding cheques — should each be identifiable. A reconciliation with an unexplained difference is not a reconciliation.

Usual failure modes

Five things that go wrong, in roughly this order of frequency

These recur across businesses of every size. None is exotic, and none reflects on the person keeping the books.

What happensWhat it distorts
Personal and business money run through one accountEvery figure on the return
Owner draws booked as an expenseProfit, understated
Sales tax collected booked as revenueRevenue and the liability owed
Payroll recorded from the net cash paidWages, taxes and withholding, all three
A reconciliation forced with a plug entryCash, and the credibility of the file

The last one deserves its own sentence. A plug entry — an adjustment posted with no support, solely to make a reconciliation agree — does not fix a difference. It hides one, and leaves a permanent mark any later reader will find.

Payroll recorded from the net is the most expensive of the five: it misstates three figures at once, and the correction has to be traced back through every pay period.

Why it matters later

A file that answers a question, or one that raises one

Estee C. Dorfman, CPA, MSA was previously employed by the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, formerly known as the National Association of Securities Dealers, as a Principal Examiner. Examination work turns on one habit: a figure is credible when it ties to a document somebody else produced, and a question mark when it does not.

That habit is what bookkeeping to trial balance is for. Books that reconcile to an outside statement answer questions; books that do not reconcile generate them — from a lender reading a covenant, from a buyer in diligence, or from the assessment process, where the normal three–year window stretches to six where a return omits gross income exceeding twenty–five per cent of the gross income stated.

Good records cost small amounts monthly. Poor ones are paid for all at once, at the worst moment, by someone under a deadline.
