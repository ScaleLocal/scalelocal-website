Service

Review and compilation.

Four levels of service share one name, and the gap between the cheapest and the dearest is a multiple rather than a percentage.

The published line

Review and compilation of small business financial statements

Dorfman & Dorfman, CPAs publishes six services. The first of them reads “Review and Compilation of small business financial statements”. Two service levels are named in that sentence, and both are offered. The sentence also fixes the subject: financial statements for small businesses.

Most people arrive at this page because somebody else has asked them for a financial statement and used a word they did not choose themselves. The word matters more than it looks. Preparation, compilation, review and audit are four separate engagements with four separate amounts of work behind them, and the price and the timetable follow from which word is written into the request.

The four levels are set out below so that a request can be read accurately before anyone commits to satisfying it.

Four levels

What separates one level from the next

The levels run in ascending order of work and of cost. Each one contains everything the level below it contains, and adds something.

LevelWhat it produces
Preparation — statements assembled from the client’s recordsNo report, no assurance
Compilation — the same assembly, plus a reading for obvious problemsA signed report, no assurance
Review — analytical procedures and enquiryLimited assurance
Audit — testing, confirmation and evidence gatheringAn opinion

The firm advertises the middle two: compilation and review. Preparation sits below compilation and produces no report at all. An audit sits above review, is a different engagement, and is not among the six services this firm publishes. If a lender or a contract requires an audited statement, that requirement should be settled in writing before anything else is discussed.

The jump in cost between compilation and review is real, because the jump in procedures is real. Nothing about a compilation report implies that the numbers were tested.

Who sets the level

The level is chosen by whoever is asking

A business rarely commissions a financial statement for its own use. The level is normally imposed from outside, by a document that already exists and can be read.

01A loan covenantThe credit agreement names the level and the delivery date. The bank’s own file has the clause.Bank
02A bonding requirementA surety underwriting a contract bond sets a level tied to the size of the work being bonded.Surety
03A landlordA commercial lease can require statements at renewal or on a change of guarantor.Lease
04An operating agreementPartnership and limited liability company agreements often fix a level for annual reporting to members.Owners

Requests are frequently pitched above the requester’s own written policy. A loan officer asks for “audited financials” as a figure of speech; the covenant behind the loan says reviewed, or says compiled, or sets a threshold the business has not crossed. Ask for the clause in writing and read it before agreeing to the larger engagement. That single question has more effect on the fee than anything else on this page.

If the clause genuinely requires an audit, say so early and go elsewhere for it.

Independence

Why the bookkeeping question comes first

Independence is required for assurance work. An accountant who keeps the books ordinarily cannot also provide assurance on them. Review carries limited assurance, so the rule bites there.

The firm separately publishes “Bookkeeping Services to trial balance”. A business that wants both that bookkeeping and a review of the resulting statements has a sequencing question to raise before the year closes, not after. Compilation does not carry assurance, which is why the same constraint does not apply in the same way, but the position should still be stated plainly at the outset rather than assumed.

Raise it in the first conversation. It is cheaper to arrange than to unwind.

Before the work starts

What has to be true of the records

Every level above preparation still begins with the client’s own accounting records. None of the four levels creates information that the books do not contain.

  • The books are closed for the period, and the trial balance balances.
  • Bank and credit card accounts are reconciled through the period end, with the reconciling items identified.
  • Accounts receivable and accounts payable agree to supporting detail.
  • Inventory, where the business carries it, has been counted or otherwise substantiated.
  • Loans, leases and owner accounts are supported by the agreements behind them.
  • The basis of accounting the statements will be presented on has been settled.

Records that fall short of this are not a refusal. They are bookkeeping work that has to happen first, and that work has its own page.
