That heading is the firm’s own, and it is a claim about how the work gets done rather than a slogan about how big anyone is. Fitzpatrick & Goguen CPAs P.C. is a family business, and its statement of what it does is one sentence long:

“Fitzpatrick & Goguen CPAs P.C. provides personalized accounting, tax, bookkeeping, and other services to a broad range of clients across the Merrimack Valley and beyond.”

Personalized is the load-bearing word. There are five people here, all of them named on the team pages with their schooling and their credentials set out so you can check them. The person who prepares your return is one of those five, and the same names tend to be on your file year after year.

Three principles, and what they mean in practice

The firm states three. They are worth spelling out, because each one carries a specific meaning inside an accounting practice that it does not carry in ordinary speech.

Trust

You hand over bank statements, payroll records, closing documents and the numbers behind your household. That only works if the person receiving them is someone you would be comfortable telling the awkward parts to — the side income, the year the books slipped, the letter from the IRS you have not opened.

Independence

An accountant’s judgement is worth something only if it is not for sale. Independence means the answer you get is the one the facts support, including when it is not the answer you were hoping for, and that no product on a shelf is quietly steering the advice.

Confidentiality

It covers everything learned in the engagement, not just what lands on a tax form — the business you are thinking of selling, the divorce, the loan that did not go through. For a CPA this is a professional obligation with a state board of accountancy and a licence behind it, not an internal policy that can be revised.

Proud members of

The firm belongs to three professional bodies. They are not the same kind of organisation, and membership in each one means something different.

American Institute of Certified Public AccountantsThe national professional body for CPAs in the United States. It sets the code of professional conduct that licensed CPAs work under, along with professional standards and continuing education requirements.AICPA
National Association of Enrolled AgentsThe national body for Enrolled Agents. An EA is a federal credential granted by the IRS, earned by examination or by former IRS employment, and it carries unlimited rights to represent taxpayers before the IRS.NAEA
Massachusetts Association of AccountantsThe state association for accounting practitioners in Massachusetts. Thomas L. Fitzpatrick IV serves on its board.MAA

If the difference between a CPA, an Enrolled Agent and an uncredentialed preparer matters to you, the guide sets it out.

The shape of an engagement

First call to filing

Nothing here is unusual, but knowing the sequence in advance makes the whole thing shorter.

1. The first conversationA call or an email. What you have, what changed this year, which entities and states are involved, and whether the work is a return, bookkeeping, or both.Phone or email
2. DocumentsRecords go into the client portal, where they stay in one place rather than scattered across email threads. The list of what to gather is on the what-to-bring page.Portal
3. Preparation and questionsThe return or the books get prepared, and you hear back on anything unclear. Missing basis, an unexplained deposit and a mismatched 1099 are the usual three.The firm
4. Review and signatureYou review the return before it goes anywhere. Under IRS e-file rules the taxpayer must sign the authorisation before the return can be transmitted, the paid preparer signs it too, and you are given a complete copy.You sign first
5. Filing and afterwardsThe return is transmitted and the copy is yours to keep. Estimated payments, an extension, or a notice that arrives in August are all handled from the same file.Year round

What this firm does not do

Some of what a website is expected to offer, this one does not, and it is better to say so than to let you hunt for it.

There is no online booking tool and no contact form. Getting started means a phone call to (978) 667-4595 or an email to office@bgoguen.com, which reaches a person rather than a queue. There is no payment page on this site either — billing and payment happen inside the client portal, along with document exchange.

The firm’s services are the three it names: bookkeeping, personal income tax planning and preparation, and small business and non-profit tax planning and preparation. Investment advisory work is not a firm service. Thomas L. Fitzpatrick IV and Brian D. Goguen are each Investment Advisor Representatives with North Atlantic Investment Partners, LLC, which is a separate company, and that is the only accurate way to describe it.

Anything you are told here you should be able to verify. That is the point of naming the people, the credentials and the bodies the firm belongs to.
