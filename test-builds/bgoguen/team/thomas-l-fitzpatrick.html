Thomas L. Fitzpatrick IV, MBA, EA, CPA

Thomas L. Fitzpatrick IV leads Fitzpatrick & Goguen CPAs P.C. The firm took its present name effective 1 January 2024, when ownership began transferring to him.

He arrived at the profession by a route that almost nobody plans. His bachelor’s degree, from UMass Dartmouth, is in marketing — not accounting. From there he took an MBA at Bowling Green State University, then earned the federal Enrolled Agent credential in 2017, passed the Series 65 in January 2020, and became a Massachusetts licensed CPA in May 2022.

Read in order, that is not a wandering path. It is one qualification stacked deliberately on the last, each added because it does something the previous one does not.

Why the EA came before the CPA

Most people assume the two credentials are a ladder, with the CPA at the top and the Enrolled Agent somewhere below it. They are better understood as two different authorities that happen to overlap.

An Enrolled Agent is a federal credential granted by the IRS, earned either by examination or by former IRS employment. What it confers is unlimited rights to represent taxpayers before the IRS — in an examination, in collection, on appeal. It is the tax credential, and it is granted by the agency the representation happens in front of.

A CPA license comes from a state board of accountancy instead. It also carries unlimited representation rights, and it adds something an EA does not have: the authority to perform attest work, and a state licensing body standing behind the professional conduct rules.

Taking the EA first meant taking the tax authority first, in the area where the work actually lands, and adding the state license afterwards. Both credentials are current. A preparer holding neither has no unlimited representation rights at all, which is the distinction that matters on the day a notice arrives.

The differences are set out at more length in the guide to CPAs and Enrolled Agents.

The degrees underneath

Marketing, then an MBA

The marketing degree is worth a sentence rather than an apology. Marketing is the discipline of starting with the person on the other side and working backwards to what they need — which is not a bad instinct to bring to a return that has to be explained to the taxpayer who signs it.

The MBA at Bowling Green State University widened the frame to general management: how a business is financed, what its numbers are telling its owner, where a decision made in March shows up in the following January. A large share of the firm’s work is small business and non-profit tax, where the return is downstream of decisions about entity type, owner compensation and timing. That is management ground as much as tax ground.

Separate from the firm

Thomas is an Investment Advisor Representative with North Atlantic Investment Partners, LLC — a separate company, which is where the Series 65 belongs. That registration sits with the separate company, not with Fitzpatrick & Goguen CPAs P.C. The firm’s services are bookkeeping, personal income tax, and small business and non-profit tax.
