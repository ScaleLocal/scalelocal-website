Management advisory.

Eleven activities the firm lists for privately-owned businesses — and the experience with those businesses that it says puts it in a position to advise.

The firm’s words

A host of management concerns.

Quoted from the firm’s own management advisory page.

“Regardless of how your business is structured, you have a host of management concerns. Because of our experience with privately-owned businesses, we are in a unique position to advise you in a number of activities.”

The activities

Eleven, as the firm lists them.

Grouped as the firm groups them, in the firm’s own order.

  • Personnel Hiring and/or Training
  • Cash Flow and Financing Projections
  • Mergers and Acquisitions
  • Loan Packaging and Debt Structuring (capital formation)
  • Reconciling Work
  • Due Diligence Services
  • Insurance Coverage Reviews (fidelity bonds, general liability)
  • Compensation and Benefit Plans (profit sharing and 401(k), pension, Section 125, deferred compensation)
  • Buy/Sell Agreements (succession planning, estate planning)
  • Internal Control Procedures
  • Business Valuations

Reproduced from the firm’s own list. Two lines on that list are unfinished vendor boilerplate rather than services, and have been left out rather than dressed up.

What holds it together

Privately-owned businesses.

Every partner’s stated competence returns to the same place: individually-owned businesses, closely-held businesses, privately-owned businesses, the private sector. The advisory list above is what that experience is for — hiring, financing, buying, selling, insuring, and eventually handing on.

Three of the eleven activities are really the same question asked at different moments: loan packaging and debt structuring when a business needs money, business valuations when someone wants to know what it is worth, and buy/sell agreements when it is going to change hands.
