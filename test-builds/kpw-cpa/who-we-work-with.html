Who the firm works with.

Seven groups, each one named by the firm itself or by the peer review report it publishes. No industry list, because the firm does not publish one.

Named groups

Seven, and no others.

This is the complete list of the kinds of organization the firm itself names, either in its own description of its work or in the peer review report it publishes.

01Privately-owned businessesThe phrase the firm uses most. Its management advisory offer opens by saying that its experience with privately-owned businesses is what puts it in a position to advise, and four of the six people here name that experience in their own lines.
02Closely-held businessesNamed in the tax competences of Kenneth J. Kolnicki, Michael J. Kolnicki and Glenn Byers — tax planning for individuals and their closely-held businesses. In practice this is the same client seen from the owner’s side of the table.
03The private sectorRichard D. Wirth states a wide range of services to private sector clients; Glenn Byers states audit services to the private sector.
04Public sector organizationsNamed by the firm as a group it serves. The peer review sample bears it out: the engagements selected included work performed under Government Auditing Standards and a compliance audit under the Single Audit Act.
05Governmental and non-profit organizationsAudit services to governmental and non-profit organizations are stated by two partners, Kenneth W. Peterson and Michael J. Eckel. This is specialized work with its own standards, and it is the work the firm’s peer review looked hardest at.
06Law firmsNamed by Kenneth W. Peterson: litigation support to law firms, including expert witness, valuation and calculation of lost wages and profits.
07Employee benefit plansNot named in the firm’s marketing at all — named in its peer review report, which states that the engagements selected for review included an audit of an employee benefit plan. Plan audits carry their own rules and their own filing deadline.
What is not here

Why there is no list of industries.

Most accounting websites carry a page of industry verticals, a dozen trades listed alphabetically, whether or not the firm has ever worked in one of them. It is easy to write and it is usually fiction.

This firm names the seven groups above. Nothing else appears on this site, because nothing else can be traced to anything the firm has said about itself. If a vertical genuinely belongs here, it belongs here with a real engagement behind it.

Ask a partner what the work actually involves.

Five of the six people here are Certified Public Accountants, and the one you speak to is the one who does the work. Say what you have; the answer will be a scope, not a brochure.
