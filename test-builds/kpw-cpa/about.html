The firm.

Kolnicki, Peterson & Wirth, LLC is a Downers Grove based firm that was started in 1974. Six people, one office, and an audit practice that gets examined by someone else.

The firm

Started in 1974, in Downers Grove.

A single office at 1400 Opus Place, and one telephone number.

“Kolnicki, Peterson & Wirth, LLC is a Downers Grove based firm that was started in 1974.” That is the firm’s own sentence, and it is corroborated by the first line of Kenneth J. Kolnicki’s experience: Kolnicki, Peterson, Wirth LLC, 1974 to present.

The name has been printed three ways over the years — Kolnicki, Peterson & Wirth, LLC; Kolnicki, Peterson, Wirth LLC; and Kolnicki, Peterson, Wirth, L.L.C., which is the form the peer review report uses. They are the same firm.

Six people work here. Five are Certified Public Accountants and are named as partners; the sixth is an Enrolled Agent and a Certified Financial Planner. There is no separate audit department and no separate tax department: the person you speak to is one of the six.

What is unusual here

The work that gets independently examined.

A practice that does public sector and benefit plan audit work is holding itself to standards a tax-only practice never meets.

Plenty of firms prepare tax returns. Fewer perform audits, and fewer again perform the particular audits this firm’s peer review sample contained: work under Government Auditing Standards, a compliance audit under the Single Audit Act, and an audit of an employee benefit plan.

Those engagements are governed by rules outside the firm’s control, read by people whose job is to find problems, and — unlike a tax return — subject to review by another CPA firm. For the year ended June 30, 2021 that review produced a rating of pass.

How an engagement starts

A conversation, then a scope.

The firm publishes no fee schedule. What a first conversation does is establish three things: what you have, who is going to read it, and therefore what level of service the work actually calls for.

For a financial statement engagement, the third question is usually settled by whoever asked for the statements. Bring their letter. It normally says audit, review or compilation somewhere in it, and that one word is the difference between three very different pieces of work.
