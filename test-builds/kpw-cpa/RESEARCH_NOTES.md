## 8a. Claims deliberately EXCLUDED from the build (adversarial QA, 2026-07-29)

An independent QA pass checked every assertion on the built site against this file. The following were removed or rewritten because they were unsupported, stale, or technically risky:

- **"The firm's home since 1974" / "Founded 1974, Downers Grove"** — source supports founding *year* only, not founding *location* or continuous occupancy. GBP identifies 1400 Opus Place as a Regus serviced suite, so the claim was likely false. Now: "Downers Grove — where the firm has practiced since 1974."
- **"Parking is on site"; "DuPage County has one of the densest concentrations of owner-operated businesses in Illinois"; turn-by-turn driving directions** — unverified facility/geography claims. Removed.
- **AICPA "more than 330,000 members"** — verbatim from their 2015 site but stale (membership has since exceeded 400,000). Number removed entirely.
- **"average tenure at a firm is measured in single-digit years"; "thousands of engagements"; "roughly a century and a half of combined practice"; "two recessions since 1974"** — all invented or arithmetically wrong. Removed.
- **"2,000+ clients over the life of the practice" / "since opening in 1974"** — source states a present client count, not a 52-year cumulative. Time qualifier removed.
- **"our position has been the same for decades" (fee policy)** — the transparent-pricing language is sourced; its decades-long provenance is not. Provenance dropped. **CLIENT-CONFIRM.**
- **"publishes monthly newsletters, offers a regular series of tax seminars, maintains an extensive tax library"** — verbatim from the 2015 site; advertising a possibly-defunct program invites requests the firm cannot fill. Removed. **CLIENT-CONFIRM before restoring.**
- **"Help with controlling inventory" as audit scope** — verbatim from their site, but describes a management function that would impair auditor independence under the AICPA Code. Rewritten as inventory *observation*, with advisory items moved to a separately-engaged advisory paragraph.
- **"newly released auditing standards require a more detailed assessment of risk"** — quarantined dated claim (§3); the standards are ~20 years old. Removed from both the audit page and the Eckel bio.
- **Tests of controls stated as invariable audit procedure** — GAAS requires *understanding* internal control, not testing it; a fully substantive approach is permitted and common for small entities. Qualified.
- **"Peer reviews are performed by CPAs selected from among the AICPA membership"** — verbatim from their site but misleading about who selects the reviewer. Rewritten.
- **"the AICPA sets audit standards"** — overbroad; AICPA sets standards for non-issuers, PCAOB for issuers. Qualified to "private-company engagements."
- **"the AICPA code of conduct governs our Illinois licenses"** — Illinois licenses are governed by the Illinois Public Accounting Act / IDFPR rules, which incorporate the Code. Rewritten to cite both.
- **"Every qualified plan" files Form 5500 / is tested** — one-participant plans below the filing threshold are exempt. Qualified to "Most."
- **"Bankers, brokers, and insurance agents all give advice that pays them"** — blanket disparagement of referral-source professions on a page bankers read. Rewritten to describe incentive structure generally.
- **"AICPA-accredited valuation analyst"** — credential collision ("valuation analyst" is NACVA's CVA term). Corrected to "Accredited in Business Valuation (ABV) by the AICPA."
- **Invented biography** — Kolnicki's founding motive, Wirth's "second generation of families he began working with," and a first-person client-questions narrative. All removed.
- **"Sectors we work in regularly"** on the privately-held page — presented KPW's client sectors, but the only sourced list is the *M&A transaction* industry list. Relabelled and aligned to the sourced 16.
- **real-estate-construction page** — real estate and construction appear in the source only as M&A transaction industries. Page reframed to lead with the sourced basis (facilities taxation, leasing, M&A industry experience) and to present bonding/job-costing as industry considerations rather than claimed service lines. **CLIENT-CONFIRM the depth of this practice.**
