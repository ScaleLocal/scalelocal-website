SALON & WELLNESS MARKETING

Beautiful work deserves to be seen. Make sure your next client finds you before your competitor.

Salons, spas, and wellness practices are visual, recurring, and booking-dependent. Seamless online booking, Instagram and TikTok presence, and review velocity are what win in 2026.

Why salon and wellness marketing is different in 2026

Online booking has gone from nice-to-have to non-negotiable — salons without integrated booking on the GBP and website lose 20–40% of available bookings to whichever competitor lets clients book without a phone call. Instagram and TikTok continue to be real, measurable client-acquisition channels in this vertical (unlike most others, where social rarely produces direct bookings) — a well-tagged Reel or before/after post in a hashtag-active local market routinely produces 5–20 new client inquiries.

The economics of salon and wellness marketing

The math is dominated by repeat visit value. A hair color client spends $90–$220 every 6–10 weeks and stays with a stylist they trust for 2–5 years. A nail client spends $35–$70 every 2–4 weeks. A med-spa injectables client spends $400–$900 every 3–4 months. The unit economics of a single first visit (typical CAC: $20–$80 for hair/nails, $80–$250 for med spa) only makes sense in the context of multi-year repeat. The marketing question is not “how do I get bookings” — it is “how do I get the first booking for someone who will rebook 15 times.”

The second reality: stylist-level loyalty matters as much as salon-level loyalty. In hair specifically, clients are often loyal to the individual stylist, not the brand on the door — which means your marketing should support individual stylists' personal portfolios, not just the salon listing. The third reality: visual content is the trust signal. A salon GBP with 60+ recent style/result photos and 4+ Instagram posts per week out-performs sparse competitors every time.

Where salon and wellness marketing usually breaks

  • No online booking link on the GBP or website. A meaningful slice of younger clients will not call — if there's no “Book Online” button, they go to a competitor that has one. Integrating Vagaro, Square, Booksy, GlossGenius, or similar into the GBP and website is usually the single biggest revenue lift available.
  • Photos are stock or stale. A salon's visual content is its product. Outdated photos signal “business in decline.” A weekly refresh of real client work compounds on the GBP and on Instagram.
  • Stylist pages don't exist. Clients are loyal to stylists. A salon with individual stylist bio/portfolio pages converts at a measurably higher rate than one with a single “Our Team” page.

What we'd do in the first 30 days

Week 1: booking integration and visual rebuild. Wire the existing booking app (Vagaro, Square, Booksy, GlossGenius) into the GBP “Book” button and a prominent website CTA. Upload 30–50 fresh photos of real client work with consent. Fix hours, service list, accepted payments, parking notes.

Week 2: individual stylist pages. For salons with 3+ stylists, a real bio page per stylist: training, specialties, before/after portfolio, social handles, direct booking link to that stylist's calendar. Lets the salon rank for stylist-name searches and recovers walk-out books when a stylist leaves.

Week 3: Instagram and TikTok cadence. 3–5 posts per week mixing real client transformations, behind-the-scenes Reels, and stylist takeovers. Tag location, hashtag the town, link to booking. Cadence beats polish in this vertical.

Week 4: review and rebook automation. Post-appointment review collection at 24 hours (when the client is happiest with the new color/cut/treatment). Rebook reminders timed to each service's typical interval. Local SEO and tightly targeted paid layer on top once the foundation is producing.

Frequently asked: salon and wellness marketing

How much should a salon spend on marketing?

Most independent salons invest 4-7% of revenue, with newer locations going higher (7-10%) for the first 6-12 months while the review base and visual catalog are being built. The math works because retained-client lifetime value is so high - a $180-every-7-weeks color client over three years is roughly $4,000.

Do I really need to be on Instagram and TikTok?

Yes for most salons and especially med spas, where social directly produces bookings. This is one of the few verticals where social isn't just brand-building - it's a measurable acquisition channel. A consistent 3-5 posts a week of real client work typically produces enough inquiries to justify the time.

How do I get my booking app to show up on Google Business Profile?

Most major booking platforms (Vagaro, Booksy, Square Appointments, GlossGenius, StyleSeat, Schedulicity) have an official GBP integration that adds a 'Book' button to your listing. If yours isn't set up, that's usually a 30-minute fix and an immediate booking-rate lift.

Should we build individual stylist pages or keep one 'Our Team' page?

Individual stylist pages, almost always. Clients are loyal to stylists, not salons, and a real bio + portfolio + direct booking link converts at a measurably higher rate. It also protects the salon when a stylist leaves - the page stays, the next stylist takes the chair.
