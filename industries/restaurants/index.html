RESTAURANT & HOSPITALITY MARKETING

Empty tables on a Tuesday night? That's a marketing problem, not a food problem.

Restaurants need consistent volume. Reviews, GBP photos, platform consistency, and menu visibility in AI search are what fill the seats in 2026.

Why restaurant marketing is different in 2026

AI search overviews now answer “where should I eat” directly — ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google's AI Overview recommend three restaurants by name before the user sees a map. The restaurants showing up have strong recent reviews, clean structured menu data, and a Google Business Profile that's actually maintained. Third-party delivery (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub) is also now a permanent lane — the smart play is to use it for new-customer acquisition and route repeat customers to your owned channels.

The economics of restaurant marketing

Restaurant margins are famously thin — industry average net margin runs 3–7%, which means every marketing dollar has to come back as several dollars of incremental revenue, fast. Industry data puts customer acquisition cost for a casual restaurant at $8–$25 for a first-time visit, against a per-visit ticket of $18–$45 and a typical repeat-customer lifetime value of $400–$1,500 over 2–4 years. The leverage is in repeat: a restaurant with 35% repeat-customer rate is dramatically more profitable than one at 15%, even with identical food.

The second reality is that reviews are the single biggest conversion factor in this vertical. Industry surveys consistently put 90%+ of restaurant customers reading reviews before choosing, and recency matters more than total count — a restaurant with 180 reviews from the last 12 months beats one with 600 from four years ago. The third reality: GBP photos and menu data drive walk-ins. Listings with 50+ recent customer photos plus 20+ owner-uploaded photos consistently out-perform sparse listings, and structured menu data (JSON-LD Menu schema) is increasingly cited by AI overviews.

Where restaurant marketing usually breaks

  • The GBP looks half-built. Hours not updated since a holiday change, no recent photos, no menu, no reservation/order link, no Q&A answers. Customers read this as “closed or sketchy” and move on.
  • The menu isn't structured for search. A PDF menu hidden behind a click is invisible to Google and AI. A real HTML menu with Menu schema ranks for dish-level searches and gets cited in AI overviews.
  • Review responses are missing or generic. Restaurants responding to every review — warmly to positives, professionally to negatives — get measurably more conversions from listing readers than those that don't.

What we'd do in the first 30 days

Week 1: GBP and platform consistency. Lock down the Google Business Profile (hours including holidays, dine-in/takeout/delivery flags, accepted payments, dietary attributes, reservation link, order link, 30+ photos), then reconcile against Yelp, TripAdvisor, OpenTable, and DoorDash so every platform shows the same info.

Week 2: structured menu and AI-search readiness. Convert the menu from PDF to real HTML with Menu schema. Each dish gets a name, description, and price. This is what gets cited when someone asks AI “does [restaurant] have [dish]” or “best [cuisine] near me with [dietary preference].” Add a dedicated photos page.

Week 3: review system at scale. Post-visit review collection via text or receipt QR code. Target 15–25 new reviews per month consistently. Respond to every single one — warmly to positives, professionally to negatives. The cadence is the biggest ongoing local-ranking lever.

Week 4: owned-channel acquisition. Simple regulars list: SMS opt-in via QR codes, low-friction loyalty, occasional “new on the menu” or “reservation availability Friday” text. Third-party delivery for new-customer acquisition; the owned channel is where margin lives.

Frequently asked: restaurant marketing

How much should a restaurant spend on marketing?

Most independent restaurants spend 3-6% of revenue - the thin margins force discipline. Newer restaurants or those rebuilding go higher (5-8%) for 6-12 months while the review base, GBP, and owned-channel list are built. Foundation work (GBP, reviews, menu structure) should never be cut, even when budget tightens.

Are third-party delivery apps worth the 25-30% commission?

Yes, as a new-customer acquisition channel. A first-time delivery customer who becomes a direct repeat over the next year pays back even after commission. The strategic move is to use third-party platforms for discovery, then route repeats to direct online ordering, your loyalty list, or dine-in.

How important are food photos on Google Business Profile?

Critical. Listings with 50+ customer photos plus 20+ owner-uploaded photos consistently out-perform sparse listings on impressions and call/direction taps. Photos shot well (good light, real dishes, real interior - not stock) are one of the highest-ROI things a restaurant can produce.

Should restaurants be on Yelp?

Yes, but as a listing to maintain, not necessarily a platform to pay for. Claiming it, keeping hours accurate, and responding to reviews matters. Paid Yelp packages are rarely positive-ROI for independents compared to running Google Search ads on the same audience.
