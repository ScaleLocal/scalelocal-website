How do cleaning businesses get more clients?

7 min read

Cleaning businesses grow by winning recurring clients, not one-time jobs, and recurring clients come down to trust because you're being handed keys to someone's home or office. The companies that fill their schedule stack glowing reviews, show real before-and-after proof, rank locally for 'house cleaning near me,' and lock in both weekly residential clients and steady commercial contracts. Sell the relationship and the consistency, not the single deep clean.

Let's name the thing that makes marketing a cleaning business different: you're asking people to hand you a key to their empty house, or the alarm code to their office after hours, and trust you with everything inside. No other trade requires quite that level of intimacy. Which means your marketing isn't really selling clean floors — it's selling trust. Get that right and clients stay for years. Get it wrong and you'll never get past the first quote.

Trust is the product — so lead with proof

A homeowner choosing a cleaner is quietly asking, "Will these people steal from me? Will they actually show up? Will they do a good job when I'm not watching?" Your marketing has to answer all three before they'll even book a first clean. The strongest answer is social proof.

  • Reviews carry enormous weight here. 97% of consumers read them before hiring, and roughly 90% trust them as much as a recommendation from a friend. For an in-home service, reviews mentioning honesty, reliability, and "I trust them in my home" are pure gold.
  • Show your team. Real photos of uniformed, smiling staff — not stock images — tell a nervous homeowner these are real, vetted people, not a faceless gig crew.
  • State the trust signals plainly: bonded, insured, background-checked. The cleaning companies that bury this lose to the ones that say it on the homepage.

Build the review habit deliberately — our guide to getting more Google reviews shows how, and since cleaning is recurring, you have a built-in reason to ask after a great visit.

Sell the recurring plan, not the one-time clean

A single deep clean is a transaction. A weekly or biweekly client is a business. The growing cleaning companies treat the first clean as a tryout for an ongoing relationship and price and pitch accordingly — "most clients move to biweekly after the first visit." Recurring residential clients give you predictable revenue and the chance to ask for reviews and referrals visit after visit. Optimize every part of your funnel toward the standing appointment, not the one-off.

Get found for "house cleaning near me"

When someone decides to hire a cleaner, they Google "house cleaning near me" or "maid service [town]" — and increasingly ask an AI tool, since 45% of consumers now use AI to find local services. Either way, you need to be visible. The Google map pack's top three results capture about 44% of clicks, so ranking there is non-negotiable. If you're not showing up at all, start with why your business isn't showing up on Google, then tighten everything with our Google Business Profile optimization guide.

Don't sleep on commercial contracts

Residential clients are bread and butter, but commercial cleaning — offices, medical suites, retail, property managers — is where you build stability. These accounts sign longer agreements, pay reliably, and a handful of them can floor your whole month with recurring revenue. Marketing to them is a different motion: it's relationship-driven, referral-heavy, and leans on a website that looks legitimate enough for a facilities manager to trust. If you doubt the site matters, we settle that in do contractors need a website in 2026.

Answer the phone — or lose the booking

Cleaning shoppers are decisive and they're comparing. Someone who calls three cleaning companies books the one that picks up and quotes confidently — the others get voicemail and get skipped. The average home-service business misses 30 to 40% of calls during peak hours, and a missed call in cleaning isn't just one job, it's a recurring client lost for years. We break down the cost in what missed calls are costing your business.

Before-and-afters work better than you think

Cleaning is visual, and people underrate it. A filthy oven turned spotless, a grimy grout line restored, a chaotic kitchen made pristine — these before-and-afters are satisfying to look at and prove your standard at a glance. Post them to your Google profile and social regularly. They're free content that does real selling, especially for move-out cleans and deep cleans where the transformation is dramatic.

Turn happy clients into a referral engine

Cleaning referrals are unusually strong because trust transfers directly — a friend saying "I let them in my house and they're great" erases a stranger's biggest fear instantly. A simple referral incentive ("give a friend a free clean, get one yourself") and consistent follow-up with your existing client list will out-perform paid ads on cost every time. Strip it all down and the playbook is short: prove you're trustworthy, get found locally, answer every call, and turn the first clean into a standing appointment. Do that consistently and a cleaning business fills up faster than almost any other trade — because once people trust you in their home, they rarely go looking for anyone else.

Frequently asked questions

How do cleaning businesses get more clients?

Lead with trust, since clients are handing you keys to their home or office. Stack reviews that mention honesty and reliability, state plainly that you are bonded, insured, and background-checked, rank locally for 'house cleaning near me,' answer every call, and convert the first clean into a recurring weekly or biweekly appointment.

Why are reviews so important for cleaning companies?

Because cleaning is an in-home service built on trust. With 97% of consumers reading reviews and about 90% trusting them like a personal recommendation, reviews that mention honesty and 'I trust them in my home' directly answer a nervous homeowner's biggest fears about letting a stranger in.

Should a cleaning business focus on residential or commercial clients?

Both, for different reasons. Recurring residential clients give steady bread-and-butter revenue and easy reviews and referrals, while commercial contracts with offices and property managers provide larger, more stable long-term agreements. A healthy cleaning business markets to both.

How do cleaning businesses get more referrals?

Cleaning referrals are powerful because trust transfers directly, so a simple incentive like 'refer a friend, you both get a free clean' plus consistent follow-up with your existing clients works extremely well.
