How do contractors show up in AI search?

9 min read

A growing share of homeowners now ask ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity to find a contractor before they ever open Google — 22% go to ChatGPT first, and AI tools recommend only about 1.2% of local businesses. You become one of the recommended few through answer-engine optimization: a steady stream of strong reviews, clear and structured service and location information, and being cited consistently across the web so AI trusts you enough to name you.

Here’s a number that should stop you cold: AI assistants recommend only about 1.2% of local businesses. The other 98.8% are never named, never suggested, simply absent from the answer. And homeowners are increasingly asking the question that triggers those answers — 22% now go to ChatGPT first when they need a contractor, and 45% of consumers use AI tools to find local services, up from just 6% a year ago. Up to 37% of buyer research is starting on ChatGPT, Perplexity, or AI-powered search.

Read that again. A homeowner with a leaking roof is no longer typing “roofer near me” into Google. They’re typing “who’s the best roofer in [town] for storm damage?” into ChatGPT — and the AI hands them two or three names. If you’re not one of them, you didn’t lose the job. You were never in the running.

AI search isn’t SEO — it’s answer-engine optimization

Traditional SEO is about ranking a page. Answer-engine optimization (AEO) is about becoming the answer. The difference matters because AI doesn’t show ten blue links and let the searcher choose — it makes the choice for them and names a winner. There is no “close enough to the top.” You’re either cited or invisible.

And this isn’t a far-off trend. Google itself is leaning in: it’s phasing out the Q&A feature on Google Business Profiles in favor of an AI-driven “Ask Maps” experience that pulls answers straight from your reviews, photos, and profile details. The signals AI uses to decide whether to recommend you are already being baked into the tools your customers use every day. We go deeper on the shift in how AI search is changing local business discovery.

What actually makes AI recommend a contractor

AI assistants don’t invent recommendations — they synthesize what the web already says about you. Feed them the right signals and you become quotable. Here’s what moves the needle.

Reviews, in volume and with substance

Reviews are the strongest signal AI leans on because they’re recent, specific, and written by real customers. A profile with 80 detailed reviews mentioning “water heater,” “same-day,” and your town gives the AI exactly the language it needs to recommend you for those searches. Thin review counts give it nothing to work with. Volume and freshness both matter — we lay out the system in how to get more Google reviews.

Crystal-clear service and location information

AI can only recommend you for what it can plainly read. If your site buries your services in vague marketing copy, the AI can’t connect you to a specific need. Spell it out in plain language: exactly what you do, exactly where you do it, and exactly who you serve. This is the same reason dedicated service and location pages matter so much — they hand AI an unambiguous answer.

Structured data the machines can parse

Behind the scenes, structured data (schema markup) labels your information so machines read it correctly — your business type, service area, hours, ratings, and services in a format AI ingests cleanly. It’s invisible to your customers and decisive to the algorithms. Most contractor sites have none of it, which is exactly why so few get cited.

Citations and consistency across the web

AI trusts a business that the wider web agrees on. The more places that mention you — consistently, with matching name, address, and phone — the more confident the model is in naming you. Inconsistent listings do the opposite: they make you look uncertain, and uncertain businesses don’t get recommended. (Yes, this is why NAP consistency suddenly matters more than ever.)

The foundation hasn’t changed — the stakes have

Notice the pattern: strong reviews, clear information, consistency, being cited everywhere. These are the same fundamentals that have always driven local visibility. AI didn’t replace them — it raised the stakes and removed the consolation prize. In the old world, being “pretty good” got you onto page one. In the AI world, being pretty good gets you left out of the answer entirely.

Your fully optimized Google Business Profile is the single most important asset here, because it’s the structured, review-rich source AI pulls from most directly. Get it complete, keep it active, and stack reviews on it. The local SEO checklist for 2026 walks through the rest.

Start before your competitors do

Here’s the opportunity hiding in that 1.2% number: almost no contractor in your market is optimizing for AI search yet. The window where being early is a genuine advantage is open right now. The contractors who build review volume, clean structured data, and consistent citations this year will be the names AI repeats next year — while everyone else wonders where their leads went.

Frequently asked questions

Do homeowners really use ChatGPT to find contractors?

Increasingly, yes. About 22% of homeowners now go to ChatGPT first when they need a contractor, and 45% of consumers use AI tools to find local services, up from just 6% a year earlier. Up to 37% of buyer research is starting on AI-powered search rather than a traditional Google query.

What is answer-engine optimization (AEO)?

Answer-engine optimization is the practice of making your business the answer that AI assistants give, rather than just ranking a page. Because tools like ChatGPT and Gemini name one or two businesses instead of showing ten links, you are either cited or invisible. AEO focuses on reviews, clear information, structured data, and consistent citations across the web.

How do I get ChatGPT or Gemini to recommend my business?

Give the AI strong signals to synthesize: a high volume of recent, detailed reviews; clear plain-language service and location information; structured data (schema markup) it can parse; and consistent mentions of your business across the web. AI recommends only about 1.2% of local businesses, so these signals are what separate the cited few from the invisible majority.

Is Google really getting rid of the Q&A feature?

Google is phasing out the question-and-answer feature on Google Business Profiles in favor of an AI-driven Ask Maps experience. Instead of customer-submitted Q&A, it generates answers from your reviews, photos, and profile details, which makes keeping that information rich and accurate more important than ever.
