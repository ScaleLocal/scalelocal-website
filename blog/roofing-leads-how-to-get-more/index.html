How do roofers get more leads?

8 min read

Roofers get more leads by building a system that captures storm-driven demand the moment it spikes and keeps the pipeline full between storms. That means owning your Google ranking and reviews, answering every call when the phone explodes after hail, running tight ads on high-intent storm keywords, and mining past customers and referrals — the most reliable roofing lead source there is. Exclusive owned leads beat shared platform leads on both cost and close rate.

Every roofer knows the rhythm. A hailstorm rolls through on a Tuesday, the phone rings forty times by Thursday, and you spend the weekend writing estimates you can’t keep up with. Then three weeks of silence. Roofing isn’t a steady business — it’s a feast-or-famine business, and that single fact should shape your entire approach to leads. The roofers who win aren’t the ones who scramble after the next storm. They’re the ones who built a system that captures the burst and keeps work coming in the dead weeks between.

Be impossible to miss when the storm hits

When hail or wind damage sends a whole neighborhood searching “roof repair near me” at once, the question isn’t whether there’s demand — it’s whether they find you. The top three map-pack results capture about 44% of all clicks and pull 126% more traffic than the listings just below them. If you’re sitting in position seven when the storm hits, you’re invisible during the exact window that matters most.

That ranking is mostly earned before the storm ever arrives. A fully built, photo-rich Google Business Profile and a steady flow of reviews are what put you in the pack. Reviews alone are roughly 20% of local ranking, and the top-three roofers average around 47 reviews versus 38 for positions seven through ten. If you keep wondering why your roofing business isn’t showing up on Google, that gap is usually the answer.

Answer the phone — all of it

Here’s the brutal math of a storm burst: demand triples for a week, and the average home-service business misses 30 to 40% of its calls during peak hours. For most trades that’s a slow leak. For a roofer in storm season, it’s pouring money into the gutter. A homeowner with a leaking roof and water in the attic does not leave a voicemail and wait — they call the next roofer on the list.

You physically can’t answer every call while you’re on a roof. That’s the point. An AI receptionist can catch, qualify, and book those storm-week callers so the surge doesn’t slip to a competitor. The real cost of letting them ring out is laid out in what missed calls are costing your business — and for high-ticket roofing jobs, a single missed call can be a five-figure loss.

Win the insurance work

A huge share of roofing leads after a storm are insurance jobs, and homeowners filing a hail claim are nervous, confused, and looking for a roofer who sounds like they’ve done this a hundred times. Position yourself as the calm expert. The roofers who win these jobs are the ones whose website, profile, and first phone call all signal “we handle insurance claims every week, we’ll walk you through it.”

  • Publish plain-English content answering what homeowners Google before they call — how claims work, what adjusters look for, signs of hail damage. This is exactly the kind of trade where blogging pays, because it answers the questions a panicked homeowner types at 9pm.
  • Show real before-and-after photos of storm repairs, not stock shingles. Proof of recent work is what separates you from out-of-state storm-chasers.
  • Make your reviews mention claims and insurance by name — ask happy customers to note that you handled the paperwork.

Stop renting leads you don’t own

Most roofers have a love-hate relationship with Angi and HomeAdvisor, and the numbers explain why. Those platforms sell each lead to three to five roofers at once, close rates sit around 10 to 15%, and shared roofing leads run roughly $80 to $120 a pop — for a contact four of your competitors are also calling. Exclusive, owned roofing leads cost more up front, around $350 to $500, but they’re yours alone and close far higher.

The deeper problem is that you’re renting, not building. Every dollar into a shared platform vanishes the moment you stop paying. Every dollar into your own ranking, reviews, and site compounds. We make the full case for switching in how to get off HomeAdvisor and Angi and own your leads, and break down where roofing dollars actually perform in the best lead sources for contractors.

Mine the dead weeks with past customers

This is the part most roofers ignore, and it’s the single most reliable lead source there is. The roofs you replaced three years ago belong to people who already trust you. Past customers and the referrals they send are, hands down, the best roofing leads you can get — warm, exclusive, and free.

Between storms, that’s your pipeline. A simple email or text to your past-customer list — offering a free inspection, a gutter cleaning, a maintenance check — turns quiet weeks into booked work and keeps you top of mind for the next referral. The roofer with 400 names on a list and a reason to reach out never has a truly dead month.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best lead source for roofers?

Past customers and referrals, consistently. They are warm, exclusive to you, and free, and they fill the quiet weeks between storms when paid demand dries up. Behind that, your own Google ranking and reviews bring in exclusive leads that compound over time, unlike shared platform leads that vanish the moment you stop paying.

Are shared roofing leads from Angi or HomeAdvisor worth it?

Usually not as your main source. Shared roofing leads run about $80 to $120 but get sold to three to five roofers at once and close at only 10 to 15%. Exclusive owned leads cost more up front, around $350 to $500, but they are yours alone and close far higher, and they build an asset you keep instead of rent.

How do roofers handle the storm-driven feast-or-famine cycle?

Capture the burst and fill the gaps. During a storm surge, rank in the map pack and answer every call, using an AI receptionist if needed since the surge triples your call volume. Between storms, work your past-customer list with inspections and maintenance offers, which keeps the pipeline full when paid demand goes quiet.

How do roofers win more insurance claim jobs?

Position yourself as the calm expert who handles claims weekly. Publish plain-English content on how hail claims and adjusters work, show real before-and-after storm repair photos instead of stock images, and ask satisfied customers to mention insurance and paperwork in their reviews. Nervous homeowners hire the roofer who clearly sounds like they have done it a hundred times.
