How missed calls are costing your local business real money

6 min read

In short: local service businesses miss far more calls than owners realize — often 20 to 30 percent — and most missed callers simply dial the next business instead of leaving a voicemail. For businesses in Tewksbury and across Greater Lowell, capturing those calls is the fastest revenue win available. This guide shows the real cost and the fix.

Here is a number that should keep you up at night: the average local service business misses 27% of incoming calls during business hours. After hours, that number jumps to nearly 100%.

Every one of those missed calls is a person who needed your service right now. And when you do not answer, they do not leave a voicemail and wait patiently. They call the next business on Google. Your competitor picks up. They get the job. You never even knew the call happened.

The math that matters

Let us say you are an HVAC company in the Merrimack Valley. Your average job is worth $800. You miss 5 calls a week — a conservative number for a busy shop. That is 20 missed calls a month.

If even 30% of those callers would have booked (and they were calling you, so the intent was there), that is 6 lost jobs per month. At $800 each, you are leaving $4,800 on the table every single month. That is $57,600 a year — from missed calls alone.

Now multiply that across every local business in Tewksbury, Lowell, Nashua, Manchester, Providence, and Warwick. The number is staggering.

When are you missing calls?

There are three windows where most calls go unanswered:

  • During active jobs. You are on a roof, under a sink, with a patient. Your hands are full. The phone rings and goes to voicemail.
  • Lunch and transitions. The 11:30am to 1:30pm window is the highest-volume call time for most service businesses — and the time your team is most likely to be unavailable.
  • After hours. 40% of local service searches happen after 5pm. These are people who just got home, discovered a problem, and are looking for help. If your phone goes to voicemail, they are calling someone who answers.

What the top-performing businesses do differently

The businesses winning in your market have solved this problem in one of three ways:

  1. Dedicated receptionist or office manager. Works if you can afford $35,000-$50,000/year in salary plus benefits. Most small businesses cannot.
  2. Answering service. A call center picks up and takes messages. Better than voicemail, but the caller knows they are not talking to your business. Conversion rates are mediocre.
  3. AI receptionist. An AI-powered system answers the call in your business name, asks qualifying questions, books appointments on your calendar, and sends you a summary — all in under 60 seconds. The caller thinks they spoke to your office. You get a qualified lead before you even know the phone rang.

Option 3 is what the fastest-growing local businesses in Massachusetts and New Hampshire are adopting right now. It costs a fraction of a human receptionist, works 24/7, never calls in sick, and does not need training.

It is not just phones

Missed calls are the most obvious leak, but the same problem exists across every channel:

  • Website chat messages that go unanswered after 5pm
  • Facebook messages with a 12-hour response time
  • Form submissions that sit in an inbox until Monday morning
  • Text messages that come in while you are on a job

The businesses that dominate their local market are not necessarily better at their craft. They are better at answering.

How to audit your own missed call rate

Before you invest in any solution, know your actual numbers:

  1. Check your phone carrier for call analytics — most show answered vs. missed
  2. Count your voicemails from the last 30 days. Each one is a missed opportunity
  3. Set up Google call tracking to see which calls come from search vs. direct
  4. Ask your team: how many calls go unanswered on a busy day? Honest answer

If the number is higher than zero, you are losing revenue.
