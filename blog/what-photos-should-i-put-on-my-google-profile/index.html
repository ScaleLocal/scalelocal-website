What photos should a contractor put on their Google profile?

6 min read

Post real photos of your own completed jobs, your branded truck and crew, your team at work, and a clean exterior or logo shot — not stock images, which homeowners spot instantly. Google now feeds its AI features directly from your profile photos, so the businesses with rich, recent, geotagged galleries get surfaced and recommended while empty profiles get skipped.

Most contractors treat their Google Business Profile photos as an afterthought — three blurry shots uploaded the day they claimed the listing, then never touched again. That used to be merely lazy. In 2026 it’s expensive. Google is phasing out the old Q&A feature and replacing it with AI-driven “Ask Maps,” a system that pulls answers straight from your reviews, your business info, and — critically — your photos. The pictures on your profile are no longer decoration. They’re ranking fuel and sales tools at the same time.

So let’s get specific about what to post.

The photos that actually earn you jobs

  • Completed-job shots (your highest-value photos). Real finished work — a re-shingled roof, a tiled bathroom, a rebuilt deck. These prove competence faster than any paragraph of copy. Aim for sharp, well-lit, straight-on shots.
  • Before-and-afters. The single most persuasive format in home services. The “before” gives the homeowner a problem they recognize; the “after” shows you fixing it. Post them as a pair.
  • Your branded truck and crew. A clean truck with your logo and a uniformed crew screams “real local company.” This is exactly the trust signal that separates you from out-of-state lead resellers with no physical presence.
  • Action shots. Your team mid-install, on a ladder, running equipment. Movement reads as a busy, working business — not a profile that was claimed once and abandoned.
  • A strong exterior or logo cover photo. Your shop, a branded vehicle, or a clean logo treatment. This is the first thing people see, so make it look established.

The photos quietly hurting you

Just as important is what to delete:

  • Stock photos. Homeowners spot a generic stock kitchen instantly, and it reads as “this company has no real work to show.” Worse than no photo.
  • Dark, blurry, or crooked shots. If the photo looks careless, buyers assume the work is too.
  • Anything with another company’s branding accidentally in frame — a competitor’s truck, a supplier’s logo on a dumpster.
  • Cluttered job sites. A messy background makes even great work look amateur. Tidy the frame before you shoot.

Quantity and freshness both matter

One great photo won’t cut it. Profiles with deep, varied galleries get more views and more calls, and they give Google’s AI features far more to pull from when someone searches. Treat photos like reviews: a steady stream, not a one-time dump.

Build a dead-simple habit — snap two or three shots at the end of every job, straight from your phone, and upload them that week. Over a few months you’ll have a gallery that makes a one-truck operation look like the most active contractor in town. That activity is exactly the kind of signal that helps you optimize your Google Business Profile and climb in the results.

A few technical wins most contractors miss

  • Shoot on your phone, on-site. Photos taken in your service area carry location data that subtly reinforces where you work — useful when you’re trying to show up on Google in the towns you serve.
  • Add photos to your services and posts, too, not just the main gallery. Every surface with a real image outperforms the same surface without one.
  • Caption nothing misleading. If Google’s AI is reading your profile to answer “who does bathroom remodels near me,” accurate, relevant images make you the answer instead of a competitor.

How to make a photo habit stick

The hardest part isn’t taking good photos — it’s remembering to upload them once the truck pulls away. So bolt it onto something you already do. Snap your shots while you’re documenting the finished job for your own records, then upload before you leave the driveway while you still have a minute. Some contractors assign it to one crew member as a standing task; others batch a week’s worth every Friday. Whatever the method, the goal is the same: never let a completed job go undocumented.

One more thing buyers quietly reward — variety. A gallery that’s forty shots of the same finished roof says less than ten shots spanning different jobs, different services, and different stages of work. Show the range of what you do, and a homeowner browsing for a bathroom remodel finds the exact proof they were looking for instead of bouncing because everything on your profile was a kitchen.

Why this is suddenly urgent

Here’s the stat that should change how you think about this: 45% of consumers now use AI tools to find local services — up from 6% a year ago — yet AI recommends only about 1.2% of local businesses. The other 98.8% never get surfaced. The profiles that win those recommendations are the ones with rich, accurate, recent content for the AI to chew on. Photos are a huge part of that content. An empty gallery isn’t neutral anymore; it’s a reason to skip you.

Pair a strong photo habit with a steady flow of fresh reviews and you give both human buyers and Google’s AI every reason to choose you.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos should I have on my Google profile?

More than most contractors think — aim for a deep, varied gallery and keep adding to it. Profiles with rich, recent photo collections get more views and calls and give Google's new AI features far more to pull from. Treat it like reviews: a steady stream of new shots beats a one-time upload, so add two or three after every job.

Are stock photos okay to use on my Google profile?

No. Homeowners recognize generic stock images instantly, and they read as a company with no real work to show, which is worse than posting nothing. Always use real photos of your own completed jobs, your crew, and your branded truck. Authentic shots build trust and feed Google's AI accurate signals about what you actually do.

What is the best type of photo for winning jobs?

Before-and-after pairs of real projects are the most persuasive format in home services, because the before shows a problem the homeowner recognizes and the after proves you solve it. Completed-job shots and photos of your branded truck and crew come next, since they prove competence and signal a legitimate local company.

Do profile photos actually affect my Google ranking?

Increasingly, yes. Google is replacing its old Q&A feature with AI-driven Ask Maps that pulls answers directly from your photos, reviews, and business info. Profiles with rich, accurate, recent images give that system more to surface, which matters when only about 1.2% of local businesses ever get recommended by AI search.
