How do tree service companies get more jobs?

8 min read

Tree service companies win jobs on two fronts at once: emergency storm work that floods in unpredictably, and planned removals and trims that book on a homeowner's timeline. The companies that stay busy capture the panic callers fast, then use before-and-after photos and reviews to convert the patient planners. Answer every storm call, show off dramatic visual proof, and rank in the map pack for both 'emergency tree removal' and 'tree trimming near me.'

A tree service runs on two completely different clocks, and most owners only manage one of them. There's the storm clock — a microburst rolls through, a 60-foot oak is leaning on someone's roof at 11 p.m., and the phone explodes. Then there's the slow clock — the homeowner who's been eyeing a dead silver maple for two years and finally decides this spring is the spring. Win both, and you never have a quiet month. Chase only the storms, and you're feast-or-famine forever.

Here's how the busy tree services actually keep the bucket truck moving.

Storm work: speed is the entire game

When a tree comes down on a house, the homeowner is not comparison shopping. They are scared, they want it gone, and they will hire whoever picks up the phone and sounds competent. The forums say it bluntly: the contractor who responds fastest with a clear, professional answer usually wins. In emergency tree work, "fastest" can mean the difference between a $4,000 job and a missed call.

The problem is that storms generate calls in bursts — thirty calls in two hours, often after dark or while your whole crew is already on a job. The average home-service business misses 30 to 40% of its calls during peak hours. For a tree service, peak hours are literally the storm. Every call that rolls to voicemail is a neighbor's tree someone else is removing. We break down the real cost in what missed calls are costing your business, and an AI receptionist can catch and triage that midnight surge so an emergency caller hears a real, confident response instead of a beep.

Planned work: this is where the margin lives

Emergency removals pay well, but they're chaos. The steady money is in scheduled work — removals of dying trees, crown thinning, stump grinding, seasonal pruning. These homeowners research before they call, and that's your opening. They're typing "tree trimming near me" and "tree removal cost [your town]" into Google, and increasingly into ChatGPT — 22% of homeowners now go to an AI tool first to find a contractor.

To win the planners, you need to actually show up when they look. That means ranking in the Google map pack, where the top three results pull around 44% of all clicks. If you're stuck on page two, you don't exist to the planned-work customer. Start with our Google Business Profile optimization guide, and if you're invisible right now, here's why your business isn't showing up on Google.

Your before-and-afters are marketing gold

Tree work is dramatic, and you're sitting on the most scroll-stopping content in home services — and barely using it. A 70-foot pine sectioned down between two houses with zero damage? A storm-mangled yard cleared in a day? Those photos and videos do more selling than any ad copy ever could.

  • Shoot every notable job — the precarious removals, the dramatic before-and-afters, the bucket-truck and crane shots. Post them to your Google profile and social weekly.
  • Video the big takedowns. A clean, controlled drop of a massive limb is the kind of clip that gets shared around a neighborhood — free local reach.
  • Caption with the win: "Removed a dead 80-ft oak hanging over a pool. No damage, cleaned up same day." That's a story a nervous homeowner sees themselves in.

Reviews are your safety credential

Homeowners are letting a stranger drop enormous, dangerous trees feet from their house and power lines. They are terrified of property damage and fly-by-night crews with no insurance. 97% read reviews before hiring, and for tree work they're scanning for one thing: "Did this crew damage anything, and did they clean up?" A wall of recent five-star reviews answering exactly that is your best salesperson. Our guide to getting more Google reviews shows how to build that habit without it feeling like begging.

Work the seasons instead of fighting them

Tree work has rhythm. Late winter and early spring are prime for dormant-season pruning and removals before leaf-out. Storm season brings the emergencies. Fall is cleanup and hazard-tree season before the snow. Smart tree services market ahead of each wave rather than reacting to it — a "book your dormant pruning now" push in February, a "get hazard trees down before storm season" message in spring.

This is also where past customers become a goldmine. The homeowner whose oak you removed last year has three more trees and a neighbor who asked who did the work. A simple seasonal email or postcard to your customer list is consistently cited as one of the best lead sources in the trades — far cheaper than buying strangers. Frequently asked questions

How do tree service companies get emergency storm jobs?

Speed wins emergency work. A homeowner with a tree on their roof hires whoever answers fast and sounds competent, so the goal is to never miss a storm-surge call. Since most home-service businesses miss 30 to 40% of calls during peak hours, and storms are peak hours, an AI receptionist or live answering service that catches and triages that burst is the single biggest lever for capturing emergency removals.

What is the best way to market planned tree removals and trimming?

Planned-work customers research before they call, so you win them by showing up in Google's map pack for searches like 'tree trimming near me' and 'tree removal cost.' Optimize your Google Business Profile, build recent reviews, and rank organically, since the top three map results capture around 44% of clicks while page two is invisible.

Should tree services post photos and video of their work?

Absolutely, it is your strongest marketing. Tree work is dramatic and visual, so before-and-after shots of cleared yards and video of controlled big-tree takedowns get shared locally and prove competence to nervous homeowners. Post to your Google profile and social every week.
