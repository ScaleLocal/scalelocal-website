How do contractors get more referrals?

7 min read

Referrals are the highest-converting, lowest-cost leads a contractor can get, but most happen by accident. You turn them into a reliable stream by doing remarkable work, asking at the right moment, making it effortless to refer you, and staying in front of past customers so you are top of mind when their neighbor needs help. A simple, repeatable process beats hoping people remember you.

Every contractor says referrals are their best source of work. They’re right — on forums from ContractorTalk to Quora, the same answer comes up again and again: past customers and referrals are the most reliable leads there are. They close faster, they haggle less, and they cost you almost nothing. So here’s the obvious question nobody likes answering: if referrals are so great, why are you leaving them to chance?

Most contractors treat referrals like weather — something that happens to them. The ones who actually get a steady flow treat them like a system. Here’s how to build one.

Step one: be worth talking about

No system rescues mediocre work. Referrals start with a job done so cleanly that the customer wants to mention you. For contractors, “remarkable” usually isn’t some grand gesture — it’s the basics done better than the last guy:

  • You showed up when you said you would.
  • You communicated before they had to chase you.
  • You left the site cleaner than you found it.
  • You handled the one thing that went sideways without drama.

That’s the bar. Clear it, and people talk. Miss it, and no referral program on earth will save you.

Step two: actually ask — at the right moment

Here’s the part most contractors skip out of awkwardness. Happy customers are glad to refer you; they just don’t think to unless you prompt them. The trick is timing. Ask when the satisfaction is highest — right after you’ve walked them through the finished work and they’ve said how great it looks. That’s the moment.

Keep it human and specific: “I’m glad you’re happy with it. I build most of my business through word of mouth — if a neighbor or friend ever needs the same work, I’d really appreciate you passing my name along.” No script feels forced when it’s genuine. Say it every single time and it compounds.

Step three: make referring you stupidly easy

A referral dies in the gap between “I should mention that contractor” and actually finding your number. Close that gap. Leave a small stack of cards. Send a follow-up text with your info and a line they can forward. The easier you make it to hand you off, the more often it happens.

Consider a light referral incentive, too — a gift card or a discount on their next service for any referral that becomes a job. It doesn’t have to be huge. It just has to say “thank you” in a way that makes people want to do it again. And the moment a referral does turn into work, tell the referrer and thank them. That single follow-through turns a one-time referrer into a repeat one.

Step four: stay in front of past customers

Most referrals are lost simply to forgetting. A customer was thrilled in March, but by the time their coworker needs a contractor in October, your name is gone. The fix is staying visible without being annoying.

A simple email newsletter or seasonal check-in does the job — a maintenance reminder before winter, a quick note when you’ve got openings, a photo of recent work. This is the single most-cited “best lead source” among contractors who’ve been around a while, and it’s nearly free. Your past customers are an asset; most contractors let it sit idle. We rank it among the strongest channels in the best lead sources for contractors.

The overlap you can’t ignore: reviews

Referrals and reviews are the same instinct expressed two ways — a happy customer vouching for you. The difference is reach. A referral helps with one person; a review helps with everyone who finds you afterward. 90% of consumers trust online reviews as much as a personal recommendation, which means a strong review profile is essentially a referral that works around the clock.

So when you ask for a referral, ask for a review in the same breath. The customer who’s happy enough to recommend you to a friend is happy enough to write two sentences online. We break down how to make that ask routine in how to get more Google reviews — and those reviews feed directly into how you show up in Google search and your Business Profile.

Don’t rely on referrals alone

One caution: referrals are the best leads, but they’re unpredictable. They come in bursts and go quiet, and you can’t scale them on command. Treat them as the high-quality core of your lead mix, then back them with visibility you control — search rankings, your website, and a way to capture leads when they arrive. The healthiest contractor pipelines run referrals and a found-online engine side by side.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to ask a customer for a referral?

Ask when satisfaction is highest, which is usually right after you walk the customer through the finished work and they have told you how happy they are. That moment of genuine appreciation is when people are most willing to pass your name along. Make it a habit on every job rather than something you do only occasionally.

Should contractors offer referral incentives?

A modest incentive often helps, such as a gift card or a discount on the referrer's next service for any referral that becomes a job. It does not need to be large; it just needs to say thank you in a way that encourages people to refer again. Always follow up to thank the referrer when their referral turns into work.

Why are referrals considered the best leads for contractors?

Referred leads arrive pre-trusted, so they close faster, negotiate less, and cost almost nothing to acquire. Contractors consistently cite past customers and referrals as their most reliable source of work. The catch is that they are unpredictable and hard to scale on demand, so they work best as the core of a lead mix that also includes visibility you control.

How do reviews relate to referrals?

Reviews are referrals that work around the clock. A referral reaches one person, while a review reaches everyone who finds you afterward, and 90% of consumers trust online reviews as much as a personal recommendation. When you ask a happy customer for a referral, ask for a review in the same conversation.
