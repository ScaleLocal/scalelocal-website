How do electricians get more jobs?

8 min read

Electricians get more jobs by getting found for the work that is surging right now — panel upgrades, EV charger installs, and whole-home rewires — while still capturing the steady stream of service calls. That means ranking locally for high-value search terms, answering every call, building reviews that prove you do specialized work, and leaning on past customers and referrals. The trade is shifting toward bigger-ticket electrical projects, and the electricians marketing for them are pulling ahead.

Electrical work used to be mostly break-fix — a dead outlet here, a tripping breaker there. That’s changing fast. EV chargers, panel upgrades, whole-home rewires, and battery and solar tie-ins are driving a wave of higher-ticket electrical demand, and the electricians who position themselves for that work are pulling away from the ones still waiting for the phone to ring with a $150 service call. Getting more jobs in 2026 means catching both: the steady service work and the bigger projects homeowners are increasingly searching for.

Get found for the work that’s booming

A homeowner who just bought an EV doesn’t know an electrician — they search “EV charger installer near me” and call from the results. Same with “electrical panel upgrade” and “200 amp service.” These are high-value, high-intent searches, and the top-three map-pack results capture about 44% of clicks. If you’re not visible for the terms tied to the booming work, you’re leaving the most profitable jobs to whoever is.

The fix is targeted and local. Your Google Business Profile should explicitly list EV charging, panel upgrades, and the specialized services you want — not just “electrician.” Dedicated pages on your site for each high-value service help you rank for those exact searches, and answering the questions homeowners Google first — “how much does a panel upgrade cost,” “do I need a permit for an EV charger” — pulls them in before they’ve picked anyone. If those searches aren’t surfacing you, start with why your business isn’t showing up on Google.

Reviews that prove you do the specialized stuff

A homeowner spending $3,000 on a panel upgrade is far more nervous than one paying $120 to swap a switch — and they scrutinize reviews accordingly. 97% of consumers read reviews, and reviews make up roughly 20% of local ranking. The top-three electricians average around 47 reviews; positions seven through ten average 38. Volume gets you ranked, but for high-value work, relevance closes the deal.

  • Ask customers who got an EV charger or panel upgrade to mention that specific job in their review. “Installed our Tesla charger cleanly in one afternoon” sells the next EV owner instantly.
  • Keep the reviews recent — a steady flow signals a busy, current business. Our guide to getting more reviews lays out a system for asking every customer without it feeling awkward.

Answer the call — service work waits for no one

Plenty of electrical jobs still start as urgent service calls: power out to half the house, a scorched outlet, a panel that’s buzzing. Those callers want help now, and the average home-service business misses 30 to 40% of calls during peak hours. Every one of those is a job — and a potential gateway to a much bigger project — handed to a competitor.

An AI receptionist catches the calls you can’t take while you’re up a ladder or under a panel, qualifies them, and books them in. That’s how a missed service call becomes a booked appointment instead of a lost one — and the math on the ones you miss is in what missed calls are costing your business.

Spend smart on leads

Electrical leads are among the cheaper trades — roughly $25 to $100 depending on the job and source — which makes the economics forgiving, but it’s no reason to overpay shared platforms. Those leads get sold to three to five electricians at once and close at just 10 to 15%. Owning your ranking and reviews brings exclusive leads that compound instead of disappearing the moment you stop paying. We break down where electrical marketing dollars actually perform in electrician marketing for more service calls and across trades in the best lead sources for contractors.

Mine past customers for the big upgrades

Here’s a goldmine most electricians sit on: every home you’ve ever wired is a candidate for the next big project. The customer whose outlets you fixed two years ago just bought an EV. The one whose lights you installed is finishing a basement. Past customers and referrals are the single most reliable lead source there is, and for electricians they’re also the warmest path to the high-ticket work.

A periodic email or text to your list — “Thinking about an EV charger?”, “Is your panel ready for today’s electrical load?” — plants the idea with people who already trust you, and trust is most of the battle on a multi-thousand-dollar job. One good list, worked consistently, turns yesterday’s service calls into tomorrow’s upgrades.

Position for the jobs, not just the trade

The electricians pulling away from the pack have stopped marketing themselves as generic “electricians” and started marketing for the specific, high-value jobs homeowners are searching for. There’s a real difference between a profile that says “residential and commercial electrical” and one that says “EV charger installation, panel upgrades, whole-home rewiring, and surge protection.” The second one shows up for the searches that matter and signals expertise in exactly the work that pays best. Lead with the booming services, prove them with relevant reviews and photos, and you become the obvious call for the jobs worth having — not just the cheap ones nobody else wanted.
